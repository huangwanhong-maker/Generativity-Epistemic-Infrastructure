# GR-130 — Generativity interchange requirements

**Publication class:** Standards-track Working Draft; not an adopted Standard  
**Maturity:** Preparatory WD 0.1, 2026-09-22; proposed requirements for review  
**Scope of this edition:** Coherent changes, recoverable acceptance and explicit exchange scope

## Foreword

The historical project outline called this work “Generativity Interchange Specification.” This Working Draft states implementation-neutral requirements under the existing GR-130 code. The technically precise Git binding belongs to GR-SPEC-130. This clarification preserves the earlier outline and follows the programme's publication taxonomy without asserting adoption.

## 1. Scope and references

This document concerns exchanges of retained record representations, modules and associated material. It proposes requirements for coherent multi-resource changes, conflict detection, retry handling, revision selection and exchange manifests. It does not define semantic truth, a universal institutional workflow, independent identity assurance, network federation or external legal validity.

Internal draft dependencies are GR-110 WD 0.3 and the bounded GR-120 WD 0.1. GR-SPEC-120 and GR-SPEC-130 provide one experimental realization. ADR-0008 records alternatives and the separation of record graphs, storage history and display projections.

## 2. Terms

**transaction:** a declared set of proposed changes submitted for one acceptance decision in a stated storage scope.

**accepted revision:** a retrievable state published by the accepting implementation after its declared validation and authority checks.

**receipt:** retained information identifying an accepted request, its attribution, reason, validation scope and resulting revision by a resolvable association.

**exchange package:** a declared selection of records and material, accompanied by sufficient scope and version information to identify included and omitted classes of content.

## 3. Proposed interchange requirements

**I130-001.** An interchange profile shall identify its version, the information model and profile it carries, supported operations, interpretation rules and limits.

**I130-002.** A transaction shall identify its request, intended base revision, proposing actor or attributable acceptance context, change reason and declared changes. Attribution shall not be presented as independent authentication unless the claimed assurance is established separately.

**I130-003.** When several changes are submitted as one transaction, acceptance shall publish their coherent resulting state or publish none of them. Validation shall resolve references against that resulting state, including records introduced by the same transaction.

**I130-004.** A transaction shall not silently overwrite a changed base. An implementation shall reject the stale proposal or perform a separately declared, inspectable reconciliation procedure.

**I130-005.** A profile supporting retry shall define logical request equivalence independently of incidental transport encoding. Repeating an accepted equivalent request shall identify its original acceptance; reusing its identifier for a different request shall be rejected.

**I130-006.** Retry recognition shall survive ordinary service restart and later accepted revisions. A receipt shall not require a circular representation in which its content includes a digest that depends on that same content.

**I130-007.** An accepted transaction shall retain its reason, attribution, declared change categories and validation scope. A mechanically produced storage change shall not automatically be classified as a recorded Event.

**I130-008.** A receiver shall verify supplied byte lengths and content identifiers against received material before accepting references that assert those values. A content digest shall not be presented as establishing the material's truth, independence or evidential sufficiency.

**I130-009.** A revision-selecting exchange shall identify one coherent revision for its records, participant references, modules and associated material. Present authorization and historical content selection shall remain distinct decisions.

**I130-010.** An export shall identify its revision, version, scope and material omissions. A snapshot shall not be labelled a complete historical backup when earlier revisions, receipts, unreferenced retained material, account records or authority mappings are excluded.

**I130-011.** A migration shall identify its source format, target format, operation and limitations; it shall preserve earlier revision access within the declared preservation policy. The receiver shall not silently add substantive meaning to earlier neutral references.

**I130-012.** A profile shall distinguish successful acceptance, conflict, unsupported content and inability to determine acceptance. A client facing an uncertain response shall retain enough request identity to determine whether retry is equivalent.

## 4. Assessment and infrastructure need

Assessment uses complete resulting-state comparison after success, induced validation/storage failures, concurrent writers, duplicate submissions before and after restart, altered reuse of an identifier, tampered bytes and historical export inspection. A positive result applies only to the exercised binding and declared scope.

The identified infrastructure need is a reusable record transaction vocabulary and acceptance binding. GR-SPEC-120 defines the former; GR-SPEC-130 defines the first binding, using Git object preparation and conditional reference publication. Git ancestry is recording provenance, not occurrence chronology or a restriction that semantic graphs be acyclic.

## 5. Open matters

Federated identity, detached signatures, offline reconciliation, loss-aware imports, physical storage quotas and independent preservation custody remain open. No compatibility with the earlier academia application's signed transition format is claimed. A later adapter needs byte-preservation and interpretation tests before any such claim.
