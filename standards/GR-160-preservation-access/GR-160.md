# GR-160 — Preservation, privacy, access and retention

**Publication class:** Standards-track Working Draft; not an adopted Standard  
**Maturity:** Preparatory WD 0.1, 2026-09-22; proposed requirements for review  
**Scope of this edition:** Associated material, access boundaries and honest retention semantics

## 1. Scope and reference basis

This bounded draft addresses retained file material associated with record revisions and the distinction between removing a current reference and erasing historical bytes. It develops the existing project outline using GR-110 WD 0.3, the programme's separation of legal and technical authority, and GR-120/130 WD 0.1.

It does not prescribe a universal retention period, legal basis, jurisdiction, evidentiary admissibility or duty to preserve. Institutional and jurisdictional decisions need their own authority and procedures. A technically valid operation cannot create that authority.

## 2. Terms

**retained material:** bytes maintained within a declared custody and access scope and referenced through a recorded descriptor.

**reference detachment:** removal of a current record's association with retained material; it does not imply erasure of historical associations or bytes.

**preservation scope:** the declared revisions, bytes, descriptors and contextual information retained under an identified arrangement.

## 3. Proposed preservation and access requirements

**A160-001.** An implementation shall distinguish mutable working files from material retained as part of a record revision and shall identify the applicable preservation scope.

**A160-002.** A retained-material descriptor shall identify the bytes, their length, descriptive filename and media type, and the recorded upload attribution and time. The implementation shall distinguish asserted media type from verified content characteristics.

**A160-003.** A replacement shall preserve the association between the earlier descriptor and earlier retained bytes within the declared history policy. A current filename shall not determine the identity of historical material.

**A160-004.** Before detachment or withdrawal, a user interface shall describe whether earlier versions and bytes remain retained. The implementation shall not label detachment as erasure when bytes remain retrievable through history or exported backups.

**A160-005.** Access to material shall be checked within its project and selected record-revision context. Knowledge of a content identifier alone shall not establish access authority.

**A160-006.** Historical content selection shall not bypass present access control. An implementation shall declare how authorization changes affect earlier revisions and already released exports.

**A160-007.** User-supplied filenames shall be treated as descriptive metadata and shall not authorize filesystem traversal, arbitrary storage paths or executable interpretation. A presentation shall declare any active-content preview capability and its protection boundary.

**A160-008.** Preservation limits shall state which resource they measure, including whether they concern current references, all retained content, physical storage or exported material. A logical content limit shall not be represented as a physical storage quota.

**A160-009.** Export and backup instructions shall identify included and excluded material, account/authority information and historical content. Restore claims shall identify the procedure and exercised evidence supporting them.

**A160-010.** Where an implementation lacks restriction, erasure, independent custody or recovery procedures, it shall declare those limitations without asserting that technical retention overrides applicable institutional or legal requirements.

## 4. Assessment and infrastructure need

Assessment includes byte verification, replacement and detachment across revisions, unauthorized identifier-based access, filenames containing path syntax, excluded unreferenced bytes in snapshot packages, included history in full bundles and observed limit failures. Declared institutional retention and restoration procedures require separate review and operation evidence.

The pilot's identified infrastructure need is an atomic association between descriptors and retained bytes. GR-SPEC-120 supplies descriptors and file operations; GR-SPEC-130 supplies bounded content-addressed Git storage. The selected first binding retains previously accepted assets, even after detachment. This explicit experimental policy is not a universal preservation requirement.

## 5. Open matters

Q-11, Q-35 and Q-39 retain erasure, sealing, restriction, backups, repair and custody questions. External material stores, sensitive use, malware inspection, storage reclamation and independently tested restoration need further work before broader deployment claims.
