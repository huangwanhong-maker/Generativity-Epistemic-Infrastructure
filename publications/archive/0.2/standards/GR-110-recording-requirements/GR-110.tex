\GSPpreface{Foreword}
This document specifies proposed requirements for generativity record packages and the practices that create, use, and maintain them. It is a preparatory Standards-track Working Draft. It is not an adopted Standard and does not establish that a formal standards project has been initiated.

Responsible editor: \texttt{UNASSIGNED}. Canonical source: \path{standards/GR-110-recording-requirements/GR-110.tex}. Intended readers are recording practitioners, participants in inquiry, record custodians, institutional users, implementers, and assessors.

Version 0.2 retains R110-001 through R110-034 and updates their dated draft dependency to GR-200 version 0.2. It develops operational planning, capture, attribution, alternatives, practical response, custody, presentation, and assessment. Normative annexes specify semantic information and assessment schedules. Informative annexes provide authored examples and a source-to-proposal explanation.

The proposed requirements are available for review and controlled exercises while programme governance develops. Their fulfillment concerns the stated recording practice and evidence. It does not certify factual truth, beneficial generativity, complete history, or external legal validity.

\GSPpreface{Introduction}
A final output can survive while the relations, judgments, alternatives, and conditions that made it possible become inaccessible. An account of a current result can also conceal mistaken assessments, changing categories, unrecognized contributions, or the loss of capacities elsewhere. This document addresses those problems through bounded and revisable recording.

A generativity package connects three distinguishable histories: changes in the situation being investigated; changes in evidence, interpretation, and judgment; and changes in categories, methods, or institutional recognition. A classification can enter the situation and alter access or action. Its practical effect does not establish that its description was warranted, and its later correction does not demonstrate repair of earlier consequences.

The recording objective is therefore more specific than accumulating observations. A reader needs to identify the asserted transformation, whose account it is, how earlier conditions are said to connect to it, which grounds support that connection, and what remains unavailable or disputed. A practice needs arrangements for proportionate collection, protection, challenge, correction, custody, and intelligible presentation.

The foundations support several forms of generative inquiry. A relation can enable, constrain, maintain, amplify, mediate, or stabilize an effect, and its role can change over time. A generative trajectory can cross several relations and carriers rather than follow one persistent object. The clauses below operationalize the recording of such accounts; they do not impose one causal theory, universal measurement scale, or exhaustive graph of possible futures.

\section{Scope}
\subsection{Included subjects}
This document specifies requirements for an identified generativity record package and the responsible recording practice. It covers purpose and boundary, material claims, record identity, time, generative accounts, contributions, alternatives, changes in distinctions, practical uptake, revision, protection, custody, and presentation.

The package contains the retained representation and its declared limits. The practice includes people or institutions, methods, assigned responsibility, collection and review actions, custody, and user-facing behavior. Full assessment of the declared scope examines both documentary material and applicable practice obligations. A package-only examination is a partial assessment under this edition; no reduced package-conformance profile is defined.

\subsection{Excluded claims and implementations}
The document does not require an exhaustive world history, unrestricted observation, a particular database, cryptographic mechanism, interchange format, common metric, or mandatory category-theoretic semantics. Narrative dossiers, tables, and technical systems can satisfy the same semantic obligations where the required distinctions and behavior remain inspectable.

It does not establish ownership, a compensation entitlement, a legal duty to disclose, or the authority of an external institution. Contextual legal and policy assertions are recorded with their grounds and status. Full GR-120, GR-130, GR-140, GR-150, GR-160, and GR-400 publications are not prerequisites for applying the bounded manual assessment in this draft.

\section{Terms and definitions}
\subsection{Generativity record package}
An identified collection assembled for a stated inquiry or practical purpose, with a scope, inventory, responsible recording practice, attributable material claims, and history relevant to that purpose.

\subsection{Recording practice}
The responsible people or organization, methods, decisions, and custody arrangements used to assemble, assess, present, and maintain a package.

\subsection{Material claim}
An assertion relied on to explain a consequential relation, generative effect, decision, classification, assessment, or revision within the declared purpose. Materiality is determined by that reliance and the selection rationale; it is not a truth status.

\subsection{Generative account}
An attributed account of how conditions or relations enabled, maintained, transformed, or constrained subsequent capacities, relations, distinctions, constraints, or possibilities. This operational term permits recording an account whose substantive warrant remains disputed.

\subsection{Generative contribution role}
The asserted target-relative function of a relation, actor, or configuration in an account, such as enabling, triggering, mediating, amplifying, stabilizing, substituting, or constraining. Roles can overlap and change; they are not new record-ontology classes.

\subsection{Generative carrier account}
An account of a persisting or transmitted condition through which an earlier contribution is connected to a later effect, including any transformation of that condition. This is a recording expression informed by FM-005, Definition 5.100, rather than a new mandatory class.

\subsection{Possibility-field account}
A bounded account of the alternatives or pathways represented as accessible, constrained, opened, or foreclosed for specified subjects under stated conditions. It does not claim to enumerate the complete possibility field of reality.

\subsection{Practical uptake}
Reliance on an identified claim, classification, or assessment in a stated action or decision, distinguished from mere receipt or availability of the information.

\subsection{Recording horizon}
The period covered by collection or reconstruction. It can differ from the period over which a claimed effect is expected or assessed.

\subsection{View}
A selected or transformed presentation of package content for an identified purpose and audience, with declared relation to source versions and material limitations.

\subsection{Declared limitation}
An explicit account of a collection, knowledge, access, method, temporal, or assessment constraint. Declaring a limitation does not automatically satisfy an obligation requiring evidence that remains missing.

\section{Planning and responsibility}
\subsection{Purpose, boundary, and selection}
\req{R110-001}{A package shall identify its purpose, intended users, subject matter, boundary, relevant time horizon, recording practice and package version.}
\req{R110-002}{The recording practice shall state the selection criteria for included material and identify known omissions material to the purpose, including unavailable perspectives, uncollected information and limits of reconstruction.}
\req{R110-003}{The package shall distinguish an inventory of retained records from an assertion that the underlying history is complete. The existence of a record shall not be used as proof of its represented content.}
\req{R110-035}{Before systematic capture or reconstruction, the recording practice shall identify the questions the package is intended to support, the decisions or uses anticipated, and the information needed to examine them. The collection plan shall distinguish necessary detail, conditional detail, and material excluded from collection with reasons.}
\req{R110-036}{The plan shall identify temporal horizon and resolution, observational boundary and scale, included perspectives, source types, expected gaps, and relevant relations outside the boundary treated as context. A change of boundary or scale material to interpretation shall be versioned and explained.}
\req{R110-037}{The practice shall assess feasible recording, assessment, protection, and participation effort for the declared purpose. If essential work exceeds available capacity, it shall narrow the scope, obtain capacity, or report an unmet requirement; resource limits shall not justify invented content or an unqualified assessment claim.}

The selection plan makes the account usable and bounded. An inquiry into a maintenance collaboration can require the work that sustained access without documenting every interaction among its participants. Selection becomes misleading when exclusions material to the stated account disappear from view.

\subsection{Responsible functions and operating conditions}
\req{R110-004}{The recording practice shall assign responsibility for capture, assessment, custody, access decisions and correction, recording combined roles and relevant conflicts. An automated component shall not substitute for an unassigned responsibility.}
\req{R110-038}{Each assigned function shall identify the responsible person or organization, accepted task, authority and access needed, relevant interests, and a contact or continuity arrangement. Where a function cannot be performed, the practice shall record the limitation and its consequence for current use or assessment.}
\req{R110-039}{The recording plan shall identify when capture is contemporaneous, retrospective, automated, or assisted and how consequential selections or transformations will be checked. Automated output shall retain its relationship to source material and accountable review.}
\req{R110-040}{A material expansion of purpose, audience, source collection, or practical use shall receive renewed scope and protection assessment before the affected activity begins. The package shall identify the change and distinguish earlier permission and evidence from authority for the new use.}
\req{R110-041}{The practice shall maintain the package inventory, current responsible functions, known dependencies, and active review or correction routes through the declared maintenance period. A decision to stop maintenance shall identify its date, reason, custodial disposition, and consequences for users.}

\section{Capture, identity, and epistemic integration}
\subsection{Intake and trace preservation}
\req{R110-042}{On incorporating material, the practice shall record its source, acquisition or creation time, received version or identifying characteristics, method of capture, relevant production context, and permitted use. Unverified source assertions shall remain attributed rather than represented as established provenance.}
\req{R110-043}{A transcription, translation, summary, selection, annotation, or reconstruction shall identify the source material, responsible transformer or method, material changes, and known loss or uncertainty. A derived account shall remain distinguishable from its source.}
\req{R110-044}{When several materials derive from a common source, the package shall retain known derivation relationships relevant to evidential independence. Multiple copies or repeated citations shall not be presented as independent corroboration without additional grounds.}
\req{R110-045}{The practice shall identify collection or extraction errors that can materially affect interpretation, including temporal aggregation, omitted context, unresolved attribution, and unsupported precision. Correction of an extraction shall not silently alter the underlying source record.}

\subsection{Package and claim dependency}
\req{R110-005}{The package shall identify its material claims and assessments as a bounded epistemic record package assessed against GR-200 Working Draft 0.2, including that draft's package-level obligations. It shall identify the assessed claim set, dependency versions, applicable requirements, evidence, exceptions and results; a claim-level check alone shall not establish fulfillment of the package dependency.}
\req{R110-046}{The assessed material-claim set shall include claims relied on for generative connections, identity, alternatives, category change, and practical consequences, not only claims supported by convenient evidence. Exclusion of a relied-on claim shall be identified as a limitation of the assessment rather than omitted from the declared set.}
\req{R110-047}{The package shall link each material claim and relevant assessment to the applicable GR-200 records and results. The dependency assessment shall cover all applicable obligations for that bounded epistemic package; successful individual claim checks shall not substitute for package-level evaluation.}

\subsection{Identity and temporal meaning}
\req{R110-006}{Each retained record and version shall be distinguishable within the package, and a reference shall identify its intended target sufficiently for the declared use. A record identifier shall not itself establish identity of the represented person, event or relation.}
\req{R110-007}{A continuity or identity judgment material to the trajectory shall identify its criterion, responsible source, supporting grounds and known dispute. Merger, succession and common labels shall not silently be treated as identity.}
\req{R110-008}{The package shall distinguish the represented occurrence or validity time from observation, recording and later assessment times when their difference is relevant. Uncertain intervals and unsupported ordering shall remain explicit.}
\req{R110-009}{Represented observations, actions, relations, states and properties shall retain the information needed to distinguish their operational roles. A relation with a material history, status or properties of its own shall be identifiable as a recordable subject.}
\req{R110-048}{A disputed identification, apparent duplicate, split, merger, or succession shall retain the alternative interpretation and the decision or uncertainty governing linkage. The practice shall distinguish correction of a record identifier from revision of a judgment about the represented subject.}
\req{R110-049}{The temporal account shall identify relevant ordering, interval, resolution, and uncertainty, including where observations leave gaps. A claimed continuous relation shall state its continuity criterion and evidence; continuity of a label, manifestation, or file shall not alone establish continuity of the relation.}
\req{R110-050}{When a reconstruction uses categories or evidence unavailable to earlier actors, it shall identify that retrospective basis and retain the earlier terminology or a supported account of it where material. The reconstruction shall not attribute later knowledge or intended outcomes to earlier actors without grounds.}

\section{Generative accounts and attribution}
\subsection{Target and connection}
\req{R110-010}{For each generative account, the package shall identify the target capacity, relation, constraint, distinction or possibility; the relevant actors or configuration; the comparison or earlier condition; and the temporal and contextual scope.}
\req{R110-011}{The account shall state how the proposed enabling, maintaining, transforming or constraining contribution connects earlier conditions to the target, with grounds, method and limitations. Temporal precedence or a diagram edge alone shall not be presented as establishing that connection.}
\req{R110-012}{An account shall distinguish the asserted occurrence or modality of an effect, such as realized, intended, possible, unrealized or disputed, from its epistemic basis, such as observed, reported, inferred or reconstructed. A realized past effect can be retrospectively inferred. The recording practice shall not manufacture an observed outcome where follow-up evidence is absent.}
\req{R110-014}{When an enabling encounter, decision or judgment is material to the account, the package shall identify its represented role, relevant conditions and the claim connecting it to subsequent developments.}
\req{R110-051}{A generative account shall distinguish the existence or occurrence claim about its target from the attribution claim connecting candidate contributors to that target. The role, boundary, scale, time horizon, and evidential limits of the attribution shall remain inspectable even when the target change is well supported.}
\req{R110-052}{Where association, sequence, or co-occurrence is used as support, the account shall state that evidential role and assess material alternative explanations, such as selection, common conditions, reverse influence, or changing definitions, or identify their unresolved status. Association shall not be relabelled as a demonstrated mechanism.}
\req{R110-053}{A mechanism account shall identify the intermediate changes proposed to carry the contribution, their connection to available evidence, and any unobserved or assumed link. Restating the final outcome as an intermediate step shall not be presented as additional evidence.}

The meaning of a contribution depends on the target. A network can be associated with income while the stated generative question concerns access to knowledge or a new collaboration. The account identifies the level at which the asserted contribution operates. Different methods can provide different kinds of support without being reduced to one confidence number.

\subsection{Alternative contributors and counterfactual reasoning}
\req{R110-054}{When a counterfactual supports attribution, the account shall identify the focal change, connected conditions allowed to vary, background conditions provisionally held stable, target comparison, time horizon, evidence for plausibility, and material uncertainty. A modeled counterfactual shall remain distinguishable from an observed comparison.}
\req{R110-055}{Where multiple or distributed contribution is material, the account shall identify the supported roles or organizational pattern, relevant complementarities, substitutions, or feedback, and the limits of allocating effects among participants. Failure to isolate a single contributor shall not force an unsupported sole attribution.}
\req{R110-056}{A claim of emergent or configurational contribution shall identify the interaction, organization, or dependency said to matter and the evidence supporting its relevance to the target. The label emergent or systemic shall not replace the account of the proposed connection.}

\subsection{Carriers, delay, and long trajectories}
\req{R110-057}{For a delayed contribution, the account shall identify the persisting or transmitted condition proposed to connect the earlier relation to the later effect, known transformations of that condition, intervening influences, and the limit of useful attribution. A long interval alone shall not establish either continuity or disconnection.}
\req{R110-058}{A trajectory containing several generative links shall preserve link-specific contributors, target, grounds, modality, and uncertainty where these differ materially. Evidence supporting one link shall not automatically validate downstream links or the whole trajectory.}
\req{R110-059}{Where available evidence ceases to support a downstream attribution, the account shall identify that boundary and classify further development as unassessed, conjectured, or separately supported as appropriate. A diagram or narrative shall not hide that change of status.}
\req{R110-060}{A path-dependence or lock-in claim shall identify the sequence-sensitive condition said to alter later alternatives, its carrier or feedback, and grounds distinguishing historical dependence from persistence under current conditions. Claimed irreversibility shall state the relevant horizon and alternatives considered.}

\subsection{Branches and contributions}
\req{R110-013}{Material participants and contributions shall be attributed with their basis and uncertainty. The recording practice shall provide a means to record disputed, collective, indirect or unknown contribution without forcing attribution to one actor.}
\req{R110-061}{An account of branching shall distinguish opening or constraining a pathway, selecting a branch, and stabilizing a later outcome when those contributions differ. Earlier enabling conditions shall not receive the whole realized history's attribution solely because they preceded it.}
\req{R110-062}{A contribution record shall distinguish descriptive attribution from acknowledgement, authorship, compensation, ownership, institutional credit, or legal responsibility. Any asserted entitlement or allocation shall identify its separate source, authority, evaluative basis, and disputed or operative status.}

\section{Alternatives, distinctions, maintenance, and loss}
\subsection{Alternative and possibility accounts}
\req{R110-015}{A recorded alternative shall state whether it was considered at the time, inferred retrospectively, modeled or merely proposed, and identify the grounds for any claim of feasibility. Recorded alternatives shall not be presented as exhaustive or equally feasible without supporting grounds.}
\req{R110-063}{A historical alternative shall identify whether it was evidenced as available to relevant actors at the time, proposed then but infeasible under identified conditions, or introduced retrospectively for analysis. A later technology, category, or institutional option shall not be represented as historically available without grounds.}
\req{R110-064}{A possibility-field account shall identify the subjects or positions concerned, the selected pathways, the relevant conditions and horizon, and the change alleged in accessibility, cost, probability, or viability. Unenumerated possibilities and limits of comparison shall remain explicit; a bounded list shall not imply exhaustion.}
\req{R110-065}{Where an option is represented as available, the account shall distinguish formal availability from practical access under material resources, authority, knowledge, timing, or participation conditions. A declared permission alone shall not establish that the affected person could exercise the option.}
\req{R110-066}{A comparison of earlier and later possibilities shall state whether the compared categories, boundaries, and subjects remain sufficiently consistent for the claimed comparison. If the process generating change or the available distinctions changed, that change shall be represented rather than forced into an unchanged scale.}

Alternatives are recorded for their relevance to the inquiry. No branch tree is required. A package can retain evidence of one abandoned option and explicitly state that other pathways were not reconstructed. A possibility claim is stronger than a statement that an observer imagined an option, and its grounds are recorded accordingly.

\subsection{Categories and historical reinterpretation}
\req{R110-016}{A material change in categories, available distinctions or methods shall identify the earlier and later formulation, the reason for change and its effect on interpretation. A new description shall not automatically be represented as a newly existing underlying object.}
\req{R110-067}{A material classification or method change shall identify its responsible source, effective or asserted period, definitions or rules changed, and consequences for reading earlier records. A later harmonized dataset or view shall preserve a route to the distinctions lost or identify that loss.}
\req{R110-068}{A retrospective interpretation shall distinguish correction of an earlier error, expansion using later evidence or categories, and subsequent transformation of the represented relation. Where more than one applies, the package shall preserve their separate grounds rather than choose one merely for a simpler history.}

The same word can refer to changing institutional arrangements, and different words can describe related practices. A genealogy can therefore connect heterogeneous predecessors without claiming one continuous identity. The package identifies the type of historical account and the limits of the continuity or formation claim.

\subsection{Maintenance, depletion, and restriction}
\req{R110-017}{Known maintenance, depletion, restriction, exclusion or loss material to the declared generative account shall be recorded alongside creation or expansion, or have an explicit collection or protection limitation under R110-002 and R110-026. The account shall distinguish descriptive effects from their evaluation for particular people or values.}
\req{R110-069}{Where maintaining a capacity or relation is material, the account shall identify the recurring work or conditions asserted to sustain it, the relevant period, and evidence or uncertainty about deterioration in their absence. Lack of a novel final output shall not be used to omit a supported maintenance contribution.}
\req{R110-070}{A loss account shall distinguish loss in the represented situation from loss of records, visibility, access, institutional recognition, or interpretability. The package shall identify which kind is asserted, its basis and affected subject, without inferring one solely from another.}
\req{R110-071}{An enabling or constraining effect that differs by participant, position, scale, or horizon shall be scoped accordingly. A short-term benefit shall not erase a recorded longer-term dependency or cost, and a loss to one participant shall not be concealed by aggregate expansion.}
\req{R110-072}{A normative evaluation of maintained, created, or lost conditions shall identify the evaluator, relevant values, affected interests, grounds, and disagreement. Descriptive generativity and the desirability of its consequences shall remain distinguishable.}

\subsection{Generativity return}
\req{R110-018}{Where generativity return is assessed or proposed, the package shall identify the asserted concept, intended recipients, scope, grounds and evaluative basis. Where it is not assessed, that limitation shall be stated without inventing a quantity or entitlement.}
\req{R110-073}{A generativity-return account shall distinguish the proposed concept and evaluation from any observed transfer, practical response, or legally asserted entitlement. If a quantity is used, the account shall state its meaning, units or scale, calculation or judgment method, horizon, assumptions, and limits on comparability.}
An account can state that generativity return was not assessed. The record of that limitation is assessed separately from any substantive claim about return. No universal distribution rule or monetary equivalent is prescribed.

\section{Practical uptake, feedback, and revision}
\subsection{Decision and intervention accounts}
\req{R110-019}{When a represented claim or classification informs a decision within scope, the package shall identify the claim version relied on, the decision, the asserted decision authority, its stated grounds and known subsequent response.}
\req{R110-074}{A practical-uptake record shall distinguish availability or receipt of a claim from actual reliance, the decision communicated, and the action implemented. Each asserted step shall identify its source and evidence or uncertainty; a delivery acknowledgement shall not establish that a judgment was accepted.}
\req{R110-075}{When an intervention rationale is within scope, the package shall identify the immediate action, intended generative effect, target relations or conditions, relevant assumptions, beneficiaries and affected interests, and evidence expected to support or challenge the rationale. It shall identify the asserted authority rather than derive that authority from the recording Standard.}
\req{R110-076}{Where monitoring or feedback is part of the assessed practice, the practice shall identify questions, observations, review points or triggers, responsible recipients, and the decisions that feedback can inform. A collection schedule without a route to consideration shall not be represented as an operative feedback arrangement.}

The practical sequence depends on context. A retrospective archive need not operate the intervention it describes. Its package can record an institution's asserted action and the evidence available about it. A practice claiming to provide an operational feedback route needs evidence that the route works within its authority.

\subsection{Learning and consequences}
\req{R110-077}{The package shall distinguish evidence that an action was implemented as intended from evidence that the anticipated transformation followed. Unexpected, delayed, mixed, or unobserved effects shall retain those statuses rather than being treated as success or failure solely from implementation completion.}
\req{R110-078}{A material feedback record shall identify the observation or testimony, its relation to the intervention rationale, the person or function considering it, and any maintained or revised action with reasons. Where the practice cannot observe consideration or action, that limit shall be reported.}
\req{R110-079}{When feedback leads to a changed target, method, boundary, objective, or evaluative criterion, the package shall preserve the change and its rationale. Later success criteria shall not be presented as though they governed the original action unless that earlier status is evidenced.}
\req{R110-080}{An account of withdrawal, reversal, or repair shall distinguish ending an activity, attempting to restore an earlier condition, and addressing residual consequences. It shall identify responsible authority, planned and performed actions, observed effects, and known limits; restoring a rule shall not alone establish restoration of the prior situation.}
\req{R110-081}{Where a corrective decision has known downstream consequences within the declared scope, the practice shall record follow-up evidence or its absence, including whether affected access, participation, capacity, or dependence changed. A corrected description alone shall not be reported as completed practical repair.}

\subsection{Revision and challenge}
\req{R110-020}{A revision shall identify its actor or responsible source, time, target, reason and affected records, distinguishing a change in the represented situation, evidence or interpretation, and descriptive regime. More than one reason can apply.}
\req{R110-021}{The recording practice shall preserve the intelligible relationship between earlier and later accounts, subject to stated retention and access rules. It shall not retrospectively present earlier actors as possessing evidence or categories first available later.}
\req{R110-022}{A correction shall identify dependent records or presentations known to the practice, actions taken to correct them and any limits on propagation. The practice shall not assert that uncontrolled external copies were corrected or deleted without evidence.}
\req{R110-023}{The practice shall provide a declared route for questions, contested attribution and correction requests, identify the responsible recipient and maintain permitted request/disposition records. Availability of the route shall not imply a legal entitlement or a promise of agreement.}
\req{R110-082}{The declared correction route shall explain how to identify the disputed material, contact the responsible function, request protected handling, and learn the status or disposition. The practice shall test the route within its claimed operating conditions and record failures and remedies.}
\req{R110-083}{Conflicting correction requests shall remain separately attributable and linked to the affected versions. The practice shall record the responsible determination or unresolved disagreement and shall not silently overwrite one account merely because another was received later.}

\section{Protection, custody, and preservation}
\subsection{Authority and proportionate collection}
\req{R110-024}{The recording practice shall declare the purpose and authority basis for collecting, retaining, accessing and disclosing material within the package, including the applicable policy or institutional context and unresolved constraints. It shall limit collected detail to that justified by the declared purpose.}
\req{R110-025}{Access and retention decisions shall distinguish content, metadata, derived views and audit traces. Preservation of history shall not automatically require public disclosure or indefinite retention of sensitive material.}
\req{R110-084}{Before collecting or disclosing sensitive material, the practice shall identify the intended use, actual authority or permission basis, audience, relevant restrictions, and feasible less intrusive information sufficient for the purpose. If the intended use lacks an established basis, that use shall not proceed merely to complete the package.}
\req{R110-085}{Protection decisions shall consider whether identifiers, relationships, timing, summaries, or combinations of metadata disclose restricted identity, existence, or content. The resulting access arrangement shall distinguish material that can be retained, examined by authorized reviewers, or presented to a particular audience.}

\subsection{Unavailable information and retention}
\req{R110-026}{Where material is unavailable, the package shall distinguish known non-collection, loss, destruction, restriction and an unknown reason to the extent that doing so is supported and permitted. It shall not disclose a protected fact merely to label an absence.}
\req{R110-086}{An unavailable-information declaration shall state only what is supported and permitted, identify its effect on interpretation or assessment, and indicate a review or future-access route where one exists. A protected omission shall not be filled with a false factual explanation.}
\req{R110-087}{The practice shall identify the custodian, retention period or event-based trigger, access-review point, and authorized disposition for material whose retention or disclosure is controlled. Expiry or withdrawal of access shall trigger review of affected claims, views, and assessment limitations.}
\req{R110-088}{When interpretability depends on a vocabulary, method, software environment, codebook, or external resource, the package shall identify the relevant version and preservation or availability arrangement. A broken reference or obsolete tool shall be assessed for semantic loss rather than treated only as a display defect.}

\subsection{Transfer and migration}
\req{R110-027}{A transfer, migration or custody change performed by the practice shall identify the source and destination versions or custodians, its authority, relevant transformation and known information loss. Unsupported finer distinctions shall not be invented during conversion.}
\req{R110-089}{Before a transfer or migration, the responsible custodian shall identify the authorized source and destination, material and versions in scope, rights and restrictions to carry forward, transformation rules, expected losses, acceptance criteria, and responsibility if the operation fails.}
\req{R110-090}{After a transfer or migration, the practice shall compare the resulting material against the declared acceptance criteria, including identity, attribution, versions, temporal distinctions, generative links, uncertainty, access restrictions, and known losses. A successful byte transfer shall not alone demonstrate preservation of meaning.}
\req{R110-091}{An interrupted or unsuccessful custody operation shall retain a record of completed and incomplete steps, authoritative copies, protection status, recovery actions, and unresolved loss. The practice shall not claim that destination custody is complete before acceptance is evidenced.}

\subsection{Correction, restriction, and disposal}
\req{R110-028}{An authorized correction, restriction, retention or disposal action shall have a scoped disposition and an accountable executor. The practice shall record permitted evidence of its result and known limitations without retaining prohibited content solely to prove deletion.}
\req{R110-092}{An authorized restriction or disposal shall identify the affected content and derived views, permitted evidence of execution, known copies or dependents, and limits of control. The practice shall review public statements and assessment claims that relied on material no longer available, without retaining prohibited information merely to document the action.}

\section{Presentation and accessible examination}
\subsection{View identity and mediation}
\req{R110-029}{A presentation supplied as part of the assessed practice shall identify its source package/version and disclose material selection, reconstruction, uncertainty, disagreement and restriction within the permitted audience. It shall distinguish an attributed claim, an assessment and an institutional determination.}
\req{R110-030}{A public or reduced view shall not imply that it contains the entire retained package. A validation or provenance result shall state what was checked and shall not be presented as certification of substantive truth or normative legitimacy.}
\req{R110-093}{Each supplied view shall identify its audience and purpose, source package and version, generation or preparation method, material selection and transformations, and update status. A cached or exported view shall not imply that it reflects later corrections without evidence of refresh.}
\req{R110-094}{A view of a generative account shall distinguish the status of the asserted effect, the evidential basis for it, and the evaluation of its desirability. A visual edge, color, badge, or position shall not be the sole means of expressing a distinction necessary for interpretation.}
\req{R110-095}{Navigation from a public or reduced view shall respect the access limits of linked evidence and metadata. Where a material link cannot be exposed, the view shall communicate the permitted limitation without suggesting that the source does not exist or that unrestricted inspection is available.}

\subsection{User tasks and support}
\req{R110-031}{The practice shall provide enough accessible explanation for intended users to locate the grounds of a material claim and the relevant review route. A practical assessment shall record observed comprehension barriers and the alternatives or limitations offered.}
\req{R110-096}{The practice shall assess whether intended users can identify a material claim and its source, distinguish assessment from institutional decision, find relevant uncertainty or alternatives, recognize revision and view limits, and locate the permitted challenge route. Assessment shall record tasks, users or representative role, observed barriers, assistance, and limits.}
\req{R110-097}{Where a presentation fails a material intended task, the practice shall provide an accessible alternative, correct the presentation, narrow the supported use, or report an unresolved limitation. Successful file rendering or text extraction shall not be reported as demonstrated user comprehension.}

\section{Assessment and claims}
\subsection{Declaration and subject coverage}
\req{R110-032}{A report shall identify the assessed subject, versions of this draft and its dependencies, purpose/profile, applicable requirements, assessor, method, evidence, results and unresolved limitations. Package-only results shall not be presented as assessment of unobserved practice.}
\req{R110-033}{The report shall distinguish fulfilled, not fulfilled, not assessed and not applicable results for each requirement, with reasons for exclusions. A failed or unassessed applicable requirement shall prevent an unqualified claim that the declared scope satisfies this draft.}
\req{R110-034}{The report shall distinguish structural or documentary checks from tests of understanding, evidential warrant and institutional performance. It shall not infer truth, complete historical recovery, beneficial generativity or legal validity from structural fulfillment.}
\req{R110-098}{Before assessment, the assessor shall identify the package baseline, recording practice, purpose, period and environment, applicable requirements, incorporated GR-100 and GR-200 versions, protection limits, assessor competence and interests, and the methods in Annex B to be used. The declaration shall distinguish planned from completed assessment.}
\req{R110-099}{An unqualified claim for the declared GR-110 scope shall require evidence for all applicable package and practice obligations, including the full applicable GR-200 package dependency. A successful documentary subset or absence of reported failures shall not establish that unobserved practice was performed.}
\req{R110-100}{A package-only or otherwise partial assessment shall identify the exact assessed subset, methods and evidence, excluded practice claims, and dependency limitations. It shall not use an unqualified label suggesting that the complete recording practice or a reduced conformance profile was assessed.}

\subsection{Methods and result rules}
\req{R110-101}{The assessor shall use requirement-level evidence and discriminating cases under Annex B. Where one identifier contains materially distinct obligations, component results shall be retained so that a fulfilled component does not conceal failure or non-assessment of another. A requirement shall be fulfilled only if every applicable component is fulfilled; any failed or unassessed applicable component shall prevent overall fulfillment of that requirement.}
\req{R110-102}{Fulfillment shall require evidence of the stated obligation under its applicability condition. A missing required declaration shall be reported as not fulfilled; inability to examine whether a represented action occurred shall be reported as not assessed unless adequate permitted evidence supports a result. Non-applicability shall identify the absent trigger.}
\req{R110-103}{Where assessment uses sampling, the report shall identify the population, selection method, covered risks, excluded cases, and limits on extension of the findings. Cases selected only because they already appear complete shall not support a general claim about an unexamined practice.}
\req{R110-104}{A dependency mismatch, unavailable required evidence, or unresolved interpretation affecting the result shall be recorded with the affected requirements and claim consequence. The assessor shall not substitute a newer dependency silently or count an unsupported exception as fulfillment.}

\subsection{Reassessment and change}
\req{R110-105}{A material change to purpose, claim set, generative account, category, protection, custody, presentation, or dependency shall trigger review of affected assessment results. The new report shall identify what was reassessed, what remains supported by prior evidence, and what can no longer be claimed.}
The assessment concerns the declared scope. It does not establish that the package contains every important historical fact or that every recorded interpretation is correct. A requirement to preserve uncertainty can be fulfilled while the underlying generative attribution remains contested.

\subsection{Absence, uncertainty, and restrictions}
\req{R110-108}{An absent scheduled element shall be distinguished as not applicable with a trigger-based reason, unknown, not collected, lost, destroyed, restricted, or otherwise unavailable only where that status is supported and permitted. An unavailable contextual factual value, including historical capture time or source-production context, may be represented by such a typed declaration identifying the responsible or attributing role, known reason, and effect on interpretation or assessment. Package and record references and versions, purpose and scope, accountable recording role, and relationship locators needed to identify asserted claims, contributions, assessments, and revisions shall not be replaced by unknown values. Required reasoning, criteria, and results of the package's own assessment shall remain explicit. Fulfillment of a contextual declaration obligation shall not establish the unavailable fact or fulfillment of an examination or action that could not be performed.}

\subsection{Method and evidence register}
\req{R110-109}{The assessor shall maintain a register for every applicable requirement, including R110-106 through R110-111, with subject/version, trigger, method, evidence locator, assessor and competence, component results, overall result, limitations, and needed action. The register shall use the discriminating assessment families in Table~\ref{tab:r110-assessment} and identify any justified equivalent method.}

Inspection examines the actual package and controlled records. A competent content review examines the grounds and distinctions asserted; it does not certify all historical truth. An observed task tests what the practice or user actually does under identified conditions. A lifecycle exercise uses authorized fixtures to assess access, revision, transfer, or disposal. Synthetic exercise results remain separate from evidence of live institutional action.

\GSPannex{informative}{Worked recording cases}
\subsection{Contested funding decision}
This synthetic case concerns a public research fund that rejects an application after an AI-assisted summary treats missing calibration metadata as evidence that calibration did not occur. The community applicants later provide testimony and a restricted maintenance log. The package contains the initial claim and its grounds, the inference from missing information, the decision that relied on it, the challenge, authorized examination of the log, and the subsequent assessment.

The revised factual assessment is distinct from the institution's decision whether to reconsider funding. A separate classification review examines whether the original scoring categories excluded relevant knowledge. The public view identifies permitted limits on the log without publishing protected content. A reader can distinguish the old judgment, current assessment, policy question, and pending practical response.

A documentary check can assess these distinctions in an authored package. Testing the practice additionally requires observing the contact route, authorized access, notifications, and reader tasks. The synthetic case does not establish that a real institution changed its decision or that interrupted research capacity was restored. The existing fixture is \path{examples/p1-contested-decision.md}.

\subsection{Maintenance, delay, and distributed contribution}
A synthetic laboratory collaboration produces little new output during a year of instrument maintenance, training, and preserving shared access. A later discovery relies partly on those capacities. The account separates evidence that the maintenance occurred, its role in sustained capacity, the later discovery, and the attributed connection between them.

Training, functioning equipment, and retained procedures are possible carriers in the account. Their supported roles can differ, and the discovery can also depend on later collaborators or changed methods. No mandatory percentage allocation follows. An earlier contributor's loss of access remains visible even if the aggregate output increases. Recognition or compensation proposals require their own evaluative and institutional grounds.

\subsection{An alternative that became visible later}
An archive contains a proposal rejected under historical eligibility rules. Years later, a new category makes its significance easier to recognize. The reconstruction preserves the earlier categories and decision context, the later interpretive method, and evidence concerning options actually available at the earlier time.

The new account can be better supported without implying that the earlier actors possessed the later concept. An alternative technologically imaginable today is labelled retrospective unless period-specific evidence supports historical availability. A claim that the original choice caused lock-in needs evidence of changed transition conditions, such as accumulated dependencies or switching costs, rather than the persistence of the chosen arrangement alone.

\subsection{Migration and loss of interpretation}
A synthetic custodian exports a package into a format that preserves current claims but omits former versions and uncertainty in temporal ordering. The files transfer successfully. Assessment nevertheless identifies loss relevant to reconstruction and treats the destination as a reduced representation.

A repair can preserve the missing relationships in an accompanying dossier without requiring one prescribed schema. The migration record identifies the source, transformed representation, remaining loss, and accepted custody. If the earlier source cannot be retained under applicable permission, the package states the resulting limitation rather than implying lossless preservation.

\GSPannex{informative}{Foundation interpretation and unresolved issues}
\subsection{Source-to-proposal relationship}
FM-005 section 5.13, pp. 105--113, develops target-relative generative attribution through association, mechanism, counterfactual reasoning, multiple contribution, distributed organization, delay, branching, and emergence. R110-051--R110-062 translate this into attributable recording of a proposed connection and its limits. The standard does not turn every account into a warranted attribution merely because the required information is present.

FM-005 section 5.14, pp. 113--122, distinguishes relational continuity from chains of generative contribution and treats path dependence, genealogy, historical evidence, alternatives, and re-epistemization. R110-049--R110-050, R110-057--R110-060, and R110-063--R110-071 preserve those distinctions operationally. The term possibility-field account denotes a bounded representation; it does not redefine the broader theoretical possibility field.

FM-003 section 3.12, pp. 129--141, treats praxis through diagnosis, enabling and constraining conditions, intervention, downstream tracing, feedback, adaptation, withdrawal, reversal, and repair. R110-074--R110-081 record the corresponding distinctions where they occur within scope. The source's sequence is heuristic; this draft does not impose one intervention lifecycle on every archive or practice.

\subsection{Open conceptual and implementation questions}
\openissue{The programme has not established a universal measure of generativity or generativity return, a mandatory causal-identification method, or exhaustive minimum historical coverage. Purpose-specific burden and usefulness require pilot evidence.}
\openissue{The relation of Property to the metamodel manuscript's Attribute and the chronology of the two Chapter 3 files remain unresolved. This draft retains operational terminology without claiming that the variants are identical or that one supersedes the other.}
\openissue{No common serialization, permanent identifier service, certification scheme, or full presentation/access profile is selected here. Reusable implementation needs enter separate Specification work items; limited assessment does not acquire full conformance by naming a future profile.}
\openissue{The required evidence for effective practical repair, independent implementation, and sustained human comprehension remains use-context dependent. An initial synthetic exercise can expose missing procedures but cannot demonstrate those outcomes generally.}
