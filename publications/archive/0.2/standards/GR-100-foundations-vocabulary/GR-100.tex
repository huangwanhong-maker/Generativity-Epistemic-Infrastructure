\GSPpreface{Introduction}
The programme responds to instability in the connection between representations, their targets, institutional classifications and practical trust. An authentic or fluent account can be mistaken for a warranted account of what happened. Correction can also erase the history of an earlier claim's influence. Inspectable recording requires these differences to remain expressible before a technical model standardizes interchange.

Reality is not reduced to what a recording institution recognizes. Manifestations, traces, accounts and judgments can disclose a target partially; they can also enter subsequent action and change conditions of further generation. A mistaken classification can remain historically important after its evidential warrant has been rejected. Its occurrence, interpretation and practical effects require distinguishable descriptions.

The foundation manuscripts distinguish abstract metamodel concepts from richer analytical categories and distinguish ontological questions from evidential access. This document uses those distinctions without turning six record roles into ultimate kinds of being. Its operational definitions do not exhaust GR theory. They support partial knowledge, contested identity, overlapping roles, changing categories and revisable interpretation.

Interoperability requires more than shared spelling. One institution can use evidence for an accepted document, another for material bearing on a claim and another for a procedural exhibit. Similarly, a numerical property can be a category code, an ordinal rating or a measurement. A common format does not make their operations equivalent. Vocabulary application therefore includes scope, source, transformation and assessment duties.

\GSPmainmatter
\section{Scope}
\subsection{General}

This document supplies a bounded operational vocabulary for recording and examining claims about generative histories. It addresses a practical problem: a record can accurately preserve an assertion while the assertion is mistaken, an authentic document can contain an unsupported inference, and a later correction can conceal the historical consequences of an earlier judgment. Inspectable practice requires distinctions among what is claimed to have happened, how it became accessible, what was recorded, what was inferred, and how the account was used.

The scope includes vocabulary use, the separation of levels of description, identity and temporal distinctions, and a candidate description of six record roles. It does not define truth, a complete metaphysics, a universal theory of evidence, legal validity, a universal generativity score, or a machine-readable schema. Domain methods can provide stronger criteria within a declared scope.

GR-110 develops trajectory recording requirements. GR-200 develops the epistemic record package. GR-120 and associated Specifications are intended to resolve technical representation after requirements and cases expose a reusable need. GR-210 is intended to address assessment and contestation procedures. References to these publications identify coordinated draft work; none is an adopted normative dependency.

\section{Terms and definitions}
\subsection{General}
The following definitions apply within this document's recording scope. Capitalized Entity, State, Event, Process, Relation and Property name the operational record roles in Clause 5. Lowercase ordinary uses remain possible when their meaning is evident. Terms concerning uncertainty and missingness describe an inquiry rather than settle the truth values of the underlying world.

These definitions support linked descriptions, not a mandatory class hierarchy. Several terms can concern one activity: observation can involve interpretation and inference, and its result can become evidence for another claim. The application identifies these functions rather than treating their coexistence as an error.

\subsection{Target}
That about which a claim is made.
\note{A target can be a situation, participant, occurrence, relation, condition, possibility, record, or another claim. Its identification can remain partial or contested. A target reference is an act of reference; it does not establish existence or identity.}

\subsection{Manifestation}
For recording purposes, an identified way in which a difference becomes available to encounter, registration, measurement, expression, or classification under specified conditions.
\note{This is a limited operational use informed by FM-004; it does not exhaust that manuscript's account of manifestation.}

\subsection{Representation}
A description or arrangement of signs used to describe, classify, model, or otherwise stand for a target.
\note{Representations can themselves become targets of inquiry and participants in later action.}

\subsection{Record}
An identified retained representation, together with the context needed to interpret its declared recording purpose.
\note{A record can contain several claims, and several records can concern one target. Retention need not mean public disclosure or indefinite preservation.}

\subsection{Record package}
A declared collection of records and contextual references presented together for a specified purpose and version.
\note{A package can contain protected components and a restricted or public view. A view's limitations are part of its declared scope.}

\subsection{Claim}
An attributed assertion with identifiable content and target, expressed under a stated scope.
\note{The assertion can concern an occurrence, a possibility, an interpretation, a value judgment, or institutional or legal status. Recording it does not endorse it. Several parties can assert the same content with different grounds.}

\subsection{Attribution}
A recorded association between a claim or activity and the person, institution, system, or source represented as originating or performing it.
\note{Attribution can be pseudonymous, unknown, disputed, or mediated through another source.}

\subsection{Observation}
A situated activity of attending to or registering a manifestation through a stated method.
\note{An observation report is a claim about that activity or its result; ``direct'' does not mean free of mediation or interpretation.}

\subsection{Inference}
A step that relates identified premises or material to a conclusion under a stated method or reasoning.
\note{An inferred claim has a different epistemic role from the claim that its premises were recorded.}

\subsection{Interpretation}
An attributed assignment of meaning, classification, or significance within a stated context.
\note{Interpretation can participate in observation and inference; these terms do not partition human or machine activities into exclusive kinds.}

\subsection{Judgment}
An attributed assessment or determination made using identified grounds and criteria.
\note{A judgment can assess evidential warrant, ethical acceptability, legal status, or institutional action. These functions carry different bases of authority.}

\subsection{Assessment}
A recorded examination of a specified question using identified criteria and material, with a result and limitations.
\note{In GR-200 an evidence assessment concerns a claim-relative evidential role. A syntax check concerns a different assessment target.}

\subsection{Uncertainty}
Specified unresolved limitations or alternatives affecting a claim's content, identification, observation, method, interpretation, timing, boundary, or warrant.
\note{An uncertainty description is itself attributed and scoped. A numerical quantity is one possible representation, not a mandatory universal measure.}

\subsection{Trace}
Material, behavior, testimony, inscription, or another manifestation identified as a remainder or consequence relevant to a target inquiry.
\note{The proposed connection to its generative conditions is a claim with its own grounds; calling an item a trace does not establish the proposed origin.}

\subsection{Evidence}
A role assigned to identified material in assessing a specified claim, with an account of relevance, reliability, and alternative explanation.
\note{The material can support, challenge, limit, or fail to discriminate the claim. Evidence is not an intrinsic status permanently attached to a file or person.}

\subsection{Evidential warrant}
The grounds offered for accepting, qualifying, or rejecting a specified claim under stated methods, assumptions, and scope.
\note{This draft records those grounds without imposing one universal sufficiency rule.}

\subsection{Provenance}
A recorded account of origins, attribution, derivation, custody, or transformations of an identified item.
\note{Provenance can be incomplete or disputed. Provenance of a claim's expression differs from evidence for the claim's content.}

\subsection{Authenticity assessment}
An assessment of whether an item is what it is represented to be, within a specified question and method.
\note{Authenticity of an utterance or document does not establish truth of its substantive claims.}

\subsection{Missingness}
A stated condition in which information sought for a recording purpose is absent or unavailable.
\note{Not observed, not recorded, lost, withheld, unknown, inapplicable, and disputed describe different conditions and are not interchangeable with a zero, false value, or non-occurrence claim.}

\subsection{Record reference}
A designation sufficient to distinguish a record or a particular version within a declared context.
\note{A reference scheme does not itself determine the identity of the represented target.}

\subsection{Identity criterion}
A stated basis for treating two appearances or descriptions as concerning the same target within an inquiry.
\note{Identity claims can be contested and criterion-dependent. Similar behavior, equal content, shared names, and common descendants do not alone establish target identity.}

\subsection{Lineage}
A directed account of derivation, succession, or continuation.
\note{Lineage can branch or merge. Its use does not automatically assert that predecessors and successors are one identical target.}

\subsection{Target time}
The time or interval a claim concerns, including an explicitly unknown or uncertain temporal location.

\subsection{Observation time}
The time at which an observation or acquisition is represented as occurring.
\note{It can differ from target time and recording time.}

\subsection{Recording time}
The time at which a record or identified version was made or entered into a recording context.
\note{It does not automatically date the target occurrence.}

\subsection{Assessment time}
The time of a stated assessment or judgment, distinct from the time to which that judgment applies.

\subsection{Descriptive regime}
The declared categories, definitions, methods, scales, or boundary choices used to describe a target within an inquiry.
\note{This is an operational term; it does not require a fixed family of regimes or a particular mathematical construction.}

\subsection{Revision}
An identified change to a representation, claim, assessment, or descriptive regime, with its relationship to an earlier formulation and the stated reason.
\note{Revision of an account of the past does not itself change the past occurrence.}

\subsection{Trajectory}
A scoped account linking conditions, occurrences, continuations, alternatives, and consequences over a declared temporal or ordering frame.
\note{A reconstructed trajectory can remain partial and contested; it need not be a single fully ordered sequence.}

\subsection{Generativity-related claim}
In this operational vocabulary, a claim about how relations, conditions, or activity generate, sustain, transform, restrict, or destroy subsequent states, capacities, relations, distinctions, or possibilities.
\note{This defines the programme's recording subject; it is not a comprehensive definition of GR generativity.}

\subsection{Generative condition}
A condition claimed to enable, constrain, or shape a specified trajectory or possibility.
\note{Its role needs attribution and grounds; contemporaneous presence alone does not establish contribution.}

\subsection{Possibility claim}
A scoped claim that a continuation was or is available, feasible, blocked, lost, or conjectured under identified conditions.
\note{An unchosen proposal differs from an evidenced feasible alternative, and each differs from a later imagined counterfactual.}

\subsection{Contribution}
An attributed participation or influence in a specified trajectory or outcome.
\note{Contribution, credit, causal responsibility, entitlement, and legal liability remain distinct questions.}

\subsection{Practical uptake}
An identified use of a representation or judgment in action, classification, allocation, or institutional decision.
\note{Its consequences can become new recording targets.}

\subsection{Generativity return}
A research and recording topic concerning claimed return pathways through which generated effects alter conditions of further generation.
\note{Its operational measurement, evaluative meaning, and any allocation rule remain \textbf{OPEN}; no universal quantity or entitlement is defined here.}

\subsection{Vocabulary application}
A named and versioned use of specified terms within a declared document, record package, profile, register or implementation description.
\note{Its boundary identifies which uses are claimed to follow this document and which belong to external or ordinary-language vocabularies.}

\subsection{Vocabulary extension}
An additional term, narrower operational meaning or application-specific relationship introduced for a declared purpose.
\note{An extension does not change the meaning of this document's terms in other applications or become a programme term merely through local use.}

\subsection{Term correspondence}
An attributed and scoped relationship between meanings in identified vocabularies or editions.
\note{Correspondence can be equivalent for a particular use, narrower, broader, overlapping, conditional or unresolved. Similar labels do not establish equivalent meanings.}

\subsection{Record-role correspondence}
An explicit relationship between descriptions using different record roles or different boundaries within one role.
\note{It identifies which targets or occurrences the descriptions concern and which information is retained, added, omitted or unresolved.}

\subsection{Actuality or modality statement}
A statement of whether a target is claimed as actual, attempted, planned, possible, hypothetical, counterfactual, unrealized or unresolved within a specified frame.
\note{These qualifications are not a universal exclusive enumeration. An attempted action can have occurred while its intended result remained unrealized.}

\subsection{Epistemic mode}
The way a claim is presented as known, reported, inferred, interpreted, reconstructed, hypothesized or stipulated.
\note{Epistemic mode differs from actuality or modality and from assessed warrant. An inference can concern an actual occurrence; a direct observation report can be disputed.}

\subsection{Definedness condition}
A condition determining whether a characteristic, classification or operation has an applicable value for a subject under its definition.
\note{Inapplicability differs from unavailable evidence about an applicable value. A representation can require both a definedness condition and a missingness reason.}

\section{Vocabulary application and semantic safeguards}
\subsection{General}
\req{V100-001}{A vocabulary application shall identify this document and version, its own boundary and version, the terms it uses, and any local extensions or narrower domain meanings. It shall describe departures explicitly and shall not claim unqualified GR-100 vocabulary conformity for a conflicting use of a defined term.}

\subsection{Reality, manifestation, and representation}


The foundation's ontological, relational, and manifestational registers concern different analytical questions. They are not exclusive categories of objects or a mandatory temporal sequence. A practice can operate before its recognition, and a classification can subsequently change the practice. This document does not require every target to receive a philosophical register label.

\req{V100-002}{A vocabulary application shall distinguish a represented target, an identified manifestation or trace, a retained record, and a claim wherever it uses those roles. It shall not treat a record identifier, representation, or manifestation as establishing the existence, complete nature, or identity of its target solely by being recorded.}

\subsection{Claim, observation, inference, and judgment}


\req{V100-003}{A vocabulary application shall distinguish assertion, observation report, inference, interpretation, and assessment where those functions occur. Where one item performs several functions, it shall identify the functions and their scopes rather than infer one function's warrant from another.}

\subsection{Trace, evidence, and provenance}


\req{V100-004}{A vocabulary application shall represent an evidential role relative to an identified claim. It shall keep provenance or authenticity results, evidential assessments, technical conformance, institutional acceptance, and normative or legal judgments distinguishable. It shall not describe success in one of these assessments as success in another without a separately stated argument.}

\subsection{Identity, time, and history}


\req{V100-005}{A vocabulary application shall distinguish record reference, target identity, and lineage. A stated identification of targets across appearances shall identify its criterion and the scope in which it is used. Unresolved identity shall remain expressible without forced merger.}

\req{V100-006}{A vocabulary application shall distinguish target, observation, recording, and assessment times wherever it uses them. It shall preserve uncertainty about time or order rather than derive occurrence order solely from record entry order.}

\req{V100-007}{An application that represents revision shall distinguish change in the target situation, change in evidence or assessment, and change in the descriptive regime. It shall allow more than one reason to apply and shall not silently replace the earlier formulation with a later one. Where earlier content cannot be retained or disclosed, it shall identify the resulting limit on historical reconstruction.}

These are three linked histories rather than three mutually exclusive event types. Publication of a revised classification can change a target situation as well as its description. A later assertion that the earlier situation differed is an epistemic revision; it does not backdate the physical act of correction.

\subsection{Generativity and practical consequences}


\req{V100-008}{An application using generativity-related terms shall identify the claimed change or continuation, affected target or participants, relevant conditions, and scope. It shall distinguish a generative attribution from a value judgment and shall not infer beneficialness, entitlement, or legitimacy from the term ``generative'' alone.}


\section{Six operational record roles}

\subsection{General}
These role descriptions are standards-specific proposals, informed by FM-007 and earlier programme vocabulary. They describe how a target is represented for an inquiry. They neither exhaust reality nor require exclusive schema classes. The term Property is retained from the programme; FM-007 uses Attribute. No equivalence between them is asserted.

In the descriptions below, \emph{minimum metadata} means the information required when the role is applied under V100-009. V100-025 distinguishes structural designations and criteria from contextual factual values that permit an explicit unknown or restriction. Common metadata means record reference and version, role, described target, recording responsibility and time, and links to relevant claims and their epistemic context. Referenced context can be shared across a package without duplication.

\req{V100-009}{An application of a six-role description shall identify the applied role, its admissibility and identity criteria, the common metadata, and the role's minimum metadata below. It shall disclose unresolved boundaries and use the temporal meaning associated with the role. Missing information shall retain its stated reason rather than be converted to a substantive negative claim.}

\req{V100-010}{An application shall allow a target to be described through more than one applicable record role without treating role overlap as evidence of inconsistency. A restriction in an application profile shall state its recording purpose and information limits. A Relation whose own identity, state, properties, or history matters to the declared inquiry shall remain independently referenceable.}

\req{V100-025}{The application shall supply its reference and version, applied term labels and definition locators. Each applied role shall have an identified role, record reference and version, target designation and responsible recording role; an unresolved target designation shall state the target question or description. These structural designations shall not be replaced by an unknown value. An unavailable contextual factual value may be represented by an explicit unknown, disputed, lost or restricted state, with the stated reason, attributing role and assessment limitation. Required admissibility or identity criteria shall be stated; a provisional assignment with unresolved criteria shall not receive a fulfilled result for a criterion-dependent obligation.}

A target designation can identify an unresolved historical subject without establishing its identity. An accountable role can use a protected reference without public disclosure of a person's name. These are supplied designations with declared limits, not empty values. Conversely, a known absence of a required criterion is a recording defect; unavailable evidence for deciding whether an identified criterion is met is an assessment limitation. A fulfilled declaration of unknown context does not resolve the underlying fact.

\subsection{Entity}
\begin{description}
\item[Definition and admissibility.] A record role for a focal subject tracked across appearances under a declared continuity criterion. Admissible when the inquiry needs cross-context or cross-time reference; use does not require an unchanging substance.
\item[Identity.] The stated reidentification criterion and evidence determine the claim of continuity. A database key identifies a record, not automatically the represented subject. A disputed criterion can remain unresolved.
\item[Temporal behavior.] Properties, membership, and relations can change while continuity is asserted. Formation, dissolution, replacement, or uncertain duration can be recorded separately.
\item[Relations to other roles.] Participation in Events and Processes; possession of described States or Properties; participation in Relations; lineage links to other Entity descriptions. These are candidate meaningful links, not an exhaustive edge schema.
\item[Minimum metadata.] Focal subject and appearances in scope; continuity criterion; temporal scope; attributed identity judgment or unresolved status.
\item[Optional metadata.] Names, identifiers assigned by other systems, formation history, known predecessors and successors.
\item[Example and counterexample.] An association tracked across a change of premises under a stated organizational continuity criterion. Two unrelated associations sharing a name are not thereby one Entity.
\item[OPEN edge.] A split into two successors can preserve lineage while leaving continuity with the predecessor disputed.
\end{description}

\subsection{State}
\begin{description}
\item[Definition and admissibility.] A record role for a scoped description of conditions of a target at a stated time or interval. Admissible when the question concerns those conditions rather than an exhaustive inventory of reality.
\item[Identity.] Same target, temporal scope, and descriptive regime identify the intended condition description; record versions and conflicting accounts remain distinct. Equality of observed values alone does not establish equality of target conditions.
\item[Temporal behavior.] A condition can persist over an interval or be known only at an observation time. Persistence between observations requires a claim. Predictive sufficiency is relative to specified questions and continuations.
\item[Relations to other roles.] Conditions of Entities or Relations; preconditions and results of Events; intermediate descriptions in Processes; values articulated through Properties.
\item[Minimum metadata.] Target; time or interval; description boundary and regime; conditions described; observation or inferential basis.
\item[Optional metadata.] Supported queries, omitted conditions, comparison horizon, warranted persistence interval.
\item[Example and counterexample.] A committee's recorded membership on a given date. A timestamped snapshot alone does not establish every member's continuing participation throughout a month.
\item[OPEN edge.] A reduced state adequate for a current report can omit historical memory needed for a later operation.
\end{description}

\subsection{Event}
\begin{description}
\item[Definition and admissibility.] A record role for an individuated happening or attempted happening under a declared occurrence criterion. Hypothetical, planned, or reconstructed events remain admissible as explicitly qualified targets of claims.
\item[Identity.] An occurrence criterion distinguishes event tokens; a description can refer to one event in several records. Repetition of the same operation or outcome does not merge occurrences.
\item[Temporal behavior.] Duration, boundaries, sequence, or overlap can be exact, interval-based, partial, or unknown. Failure or interruption does not imply non-occurrence of an attempt.
\item[Relations to other roles.] Participants represented as Entities or Relations; changes between States; steps of Processes; Properties of the occurrence.
\item[Minimum metadata.] Proposed happening; occurrence and individuation criteria; claimed actuality or modality, such as occurred, attempted, planned, hypothetical, or unresolved; separate epistemic basis, such as reported, observed, or retrospectively inferred; temporal scope; participating targets where known. A retrospectively inferred event can be claimed to have actually occurred.
\item[Optional metadata.] Place, before/after conditions, attempted purpose, completion status, links to repeated occurrences.
\item[Example and counterexample.] Two separately evidenced permission issuances with the same final status. A formula for the effect of an issuance does not establish that either occurred.
\item[OPEN edge.] Different temporal resolutions can treat one prolonged happening as an Event, Process, or several Events; the segmentation rationale matters.
\end{description}

\subsection{Process}
\begin{description}
\item[Definition and admissibility.] A record role for activity linked by a declared ordering and criterion of unity. Admissible for goal-directed, recurrent, interrupted, branching, or otherwise connected activity; achievement of an intended endpoint is not required.
\item[Identity.] The stated grouping and boundary criterion individuates the process. Equal endpoints or equal modeled composite effects do not establish the same process history.
\item[Temporal behavior.] Ordering can be partial; duration and completion can be uncertain. Alternative possible paths remain distinct from the reconstructed actual path.
\item[Relations to other roles.] Comprises or links Events and intermediate States; involves Entities and Relations; has scoped Properties; connects to dependent Processes.
\item[Minimum metadata.] Unity and boundary criterion; activity in scope; supported ordering; claimed actuality or modality, distinguished from its observational or reconstructive basis; time scope and completion/continuation status.
\item[Optional metadata.] Intended purpose, branches, interruptions, excluded steps, retained intermediate conditions.
\item[Example and counterexample.] A proposal--revision--authorization sequence with a recorded failed earlier attempt. A factorization of an outcome into mathematically possible operations is not evidence of that history.
\item[OPEN edge.] Overlapping practices can belong to several processes; no universal segmentation rule is proposed.
\end{description}

\subsection{Relation}
\begin{description}
\item[Definition and admissibility.] A record role for an identified connection, arrangement, or dependence among specified targets or conditions within a stated context. Admissible when its incidence, formation, continuity, or effects are relevant. Potential efficacy requires a further claim.
\item[Identity.] A stated criterion distinguishes a continuing arrangement from a renewed or separate one. Equal participants and equal current effects do not alone establish the same relation.
\item[Temporal behavior.] Formation, persistence, dormancy, change of terms, and ending are separately attributable. A relation can change while participants remain identified.
\item[Relations to other roles.] Participants can be targets described by any relevant role, including other Relations; Events can form or change it; Processes describe its history; States and Properties describe its conditions.
\item[Minimum metadata.] Relation reference; participant or condition references with roles; context; relation criterion and temporal scope; stated identity basis.
\item[Optional metadata.] Formation history, dependency claims, terms, changing states, prospective effect comparisons.
\item[Example and counterexample.] Two separately identified agreements between the same parties, each with its own renewal history. Co-occurrence in a table alone does not establish cooperation, trust, or dependence.
\item[OPEN edge.] Uncertain or changing participants can prevent a complete incidence description without eliminating the recorded relation claim.
\end{description}

\subsection{Property}
\begin{description}
\item[Definition and admissibility.] Provisionally, a record role for an attributed characteristic or value of a specified subject under a stated definition, time, and context. Admissible when the meaning and subject of the attribution are explicit. This operational use does not resolve the broader Property/Attribute relationship.
\item[Identity.] A property assertion is distinguished by subject, characteristic definition, contextual and temporal scope, attribution, and version. A type of characteristic differs from a particular assignment of its value.
\item[Temporal behavior.] A value can change with the subject; a later correction can instead revise knowledge of an earlier value. The two histories remain distinguishable.
\item[Relations to other roles.] Describes a target represented through any role; measurement or classification can be linked to an Event or Process; multiple conflicting assignments can concern one subject.
\item[Minimum metadata.] Subject; characteristic definition; value or explicit missingness state; time and context; method or attribution; scale and unit when used.
\item[Optional metadata.] Measurement uncertainty, valid domain, derivation, calibration reference, alternatives.
\item[Example and counterexample.] A reported resource quantity for an organization at a specified time. A numbered nominal category is not a measurement permitting arithmetic solely because its code is numeric.
\item[OPEN edge.] FM-007's Attribute has explicit domain, interpretation, scale, and definedness conditions. Equivalence to Property, separation, or renaming needs source comparison and a recorded decision.
\end{description}


\subsection{Role boundaries and admissibility decisions}
Admissibility concerns whether a description can perform the stated recording role. It does not establish the claim about its target. A proposed historical Entity can be admissible as the subject of inquiry while its identity is contested. A hypothetical Event can support counterfactual analysis while its modality excludes it from the asserted actual history. Role metadata and epistemic context preserve both distinctions.

A role's minimum metadata is semantic information, not a prescribed collection of database columns. Shared references can supply context when their version and scope are clear. A narrative dossier can supply information through identified passages. An empty field, unexplained code or link to a changing resource does not necessarily supply an inspectable meaning.

An unknown value can satisfy a duty to disclose unavailable context where a requirement permits it. It does not satisfy a duty to demonstrate continuity, observed occurrence or contribution. A protected identity can use a stable reference without publishing a name; protection does not establish that two references identify one person.

An agreement can be a Relation while tracked as an Entity for continuity. Its negotiation can be a Process; its signing an Event; its operative terms at a time a State described through Properties. These descriptions coordinate different questions. They need not assert six independently existing objects or duplicate a single occurrence six times. A technical profile that restricts overlap needs a route for retaining the distinctions relevant to its declared use.

\section{Boundaries between layers}
\subsection{General}

\req{V100-011}{A vocabulary application shall distinguish operational record roles from metamodel concepts. It shall not identify a formal carrier state with a record State, a mechanism with a Process, or formal change with an Event without an explicit scoped mapping and argument.}

\req{V100-012}{A vocabulary application shall keep descriptive, epistemic, evaluative, legal, institutional, and technical assertions distinguishable by their stated question and grounds. It shall not represent technical conformance as automatically creating factual truth, rights, duties, liability, admissibility, jurisdiction, or institutional authority.}

The same dossier can connect these assertions. For example, an institutional decision can cite an evidential assessment and an asserted rule, while each remains separately attributable and open to the appropriate form of review. This is separation of questions and authority, not a requirement for independent databases.


\section{Vocabulary management and correspondences}
\subsection{General}
Vocabulary management concerns inspectable meaning across local uses, languages, profiles and versions. A register can be a small table or a documented specification mapping. It need not duplicate a dictionary, but an exact definition and its application scope need to be recoverable. When a source is unavailable, the adopted local wording and that limitation remain visible.

\subsection{Terms, sources and local extensions}
\req{V100-013}{The application shall maintain a term-use register identifying the terms used, their definitions or dated definition references, their recording scope, their source status and the role responsible for interpreting local use. An imported label shall not establish equivalence without an identified correspondence and rationale.}

\req{V100-014}{A local extension shall identify its purpose, definition, admissibility conditions, relation to existing terms, affected uses and limitations. A conflicting meaning shall use a distinct qualification or label and shall not be presented as an unchanged GR-100 definition.}

An extension can address a specialized institutional process without claiming that its categories are universal. For example, a profile can define a reviewed submission within its own procedure. It identifies the relationship to Record and Claim, the criterion for entering the review category and the information the category omits. The label does not imply that the submission is true or that every recording context has the same procedure.

\subsection{Languages, editions and mapping}
\req{V100-015}{A term correspondence across languages, domains or editions shall identify the source and target meanings, direction, scope, responsible attribution and known non-equivalence. A narrower, broader, partial or conditional correspondence shall not be represented as unrestricted equivalence.}

A translation can preserve an operational distinction even when ordinary words carry additional meanings in one language. The operational definition travels with the label. Where a translation is contested, alternative formulations and grounds remain available. A terminology register need not require every language to borrow the same word or every application to create a universal dictionary.

Renaming a field does not by itself change meaning. Retaining the same label while changing its definition can change the descriptive regime. The register identifies both situations. A mapping that loses information describes the uses for which the mapped representation remains adequate and the uses it no longer supports. Switching to a finer vocabulary does not recreate distinctions absent from the source material.

\subsection{Modality, epistemic function and status expressions}
\req{V100-016}{An application describing an occurrence or possibility shall distinguish its asserted actuality or modality, the epistemic mode of the assertion and the assessment disposition. It shall preserve combinations such as an actual occurrence inferred retrospectively and a directly reported observation whose accuracy is disputed.}

\req{V100-017}{Where an application uses fact, verified, valid, confirmed or an equivalent status expression, it shall state the question resolved, applicable criterion, attributed assessor and grounds or assessment reference. An unspecified status expression shall not erase known uncertainty, an unresolved identity question or the limited scope of a check.}

Recorded fact can mean a statement treated as settled by an institution, an asserted occurrence, or a database entry. This document does not make these meanings interchangeable. A historical account can preserve that a claim was institutionally accepted without endorsing its substantive content. Similarly, a valid structure can contain a false claim; a verified signature can authenticate a mistaken statement.

A transformation shall not silently create target identity, occurrence multiplicity or historical detail unsupported by its source.}

\subsection{Missingness and definedness}
\req{V100-019}{An application shall distinguish missingness and definedness states material to its use, including zero or false values, unknown values, non-observation, non-collection, inapplicability, disputed assignment and restricted access where present. A mapping that combines these states shall state the loss and shall not infer a substantive negative claim from the combined code.}

Unknown is an epistemic condition rather than a third truth value imposed on the world. Inapplicable means the stated characteristic lacks an applicable value under its definition. Not measured means an applicable measurement was not performed. Withheld concerns access and can coexist with a known value in a protected view. Disputed can describe incompatible assignments with available evidence. These differences affect later counting, assessment and revision.

An application need not claim knowledge of why a value is absent. Unknown reason is legitimate when supported. It also need not reveal protected material's existence to an unauthorized audience. A reduced view can state its collection and access scope while a protected accountable record retains a more specific explanation where permitted.

\subsection{Properties, scales and aggregates}
\req{V100-020}{A numerical Property description shall identify its characteristic definition, applicable domain, scale meaning and unit where relevant. Any arithmetic, weighting or aggregation relied on shall state its interpretation, included population or components and material limitations. Numerical labels alone shall not establish the validity of the operation.}

A nominal code supports differentiation, not automatically addition. An ordinal rating supports ordering under its interpretation, not automatically meaningful distances or averages. A measurement with a unit still depends on an instrument, boundary and procedure. The distinctions remain relevant when values have the same software numeric datatype.

An aggregate can change because underlying values change, because composition or weights change, or because a classification rule changes. These are different interpretive questions. A rate can require its numerator, denominator, inclusion criteria and period for interpretation. A pooled increase does not establish improvement for every group; a pooled decrease does not by itself establish causal deterioration in practice. The inference needs its own grounds.

\subsection{State sufficiency and retained history}
\req{V100-021}{An assertion that a State description is sufficient shall identify the question or operation family, comparison horizon, relevant observations, assumptions and known omitted distinctions. Sufficiency for a current report or specified prediction shall not be represented as historical completeness or sufficiency for undeclared continuations.}

Two subjects can have the same current reported flag while retaining different conditions that affect a future response. Conversely, a reduced description can predict a selected response while omitting whether the condition arose by agreement or imposition. Predictive and historical purposes need different evidence. A statement of adequacy identifies the question answered, rather than converting that question into a universal account of the subject.

\subsection{Identity decisions}
\req{V100-022}{A relied-on identity judgment shall state whether the proposed relation is identity, similarity, identifier correspondence, succession or another defined relation, and shall preserve material competing criteria and unresolved cases. Automated matching or shared lineage shall not be promoted to target identity without the stated criterion and its supporting attribution.}

Organizational registration, membership continuity, operational practice and historical self-description can support different identity inquiries. Agreement under one criterion need not resolve another. A split or merger can be represented by directed lineage without identifying each successor with every predecessor. A shared name does not decide which relation is intended.

Identity also differs from access. Redaction can hide information needed to assess a match while a custodian retains it. A public view can report a scoped assertion and its limits without claiming independent external verification. Lack of disclosure does not justify inventing either identity or non-identity.

\section{Conformance}
\subsection{General}
The conformance subject is a declared vocabulary application at an identified edition and boundary. Requirements concerning extensions, numerical Properties, transformations or particular roles are conditional on those features being present. An absent feature can make its specific requirement not applicable. It does not exempt a general safeguard already used elsewhere in the application.

\subsection{Declaration and assessment boundary}
\req{V100-023}{A conformance statement shall identify the application, edition, boundary, GR-100 edition, applied terms, applicable requirements, assessor and evidence. It shall distinguish full fulfillment of applicable requirements from partial assessment and shall not imply assessment of excluded uses or unavailable evidence.}

A scope can be small without being vague. A short profile using only Record, Claim, Trace and Evidence identifies those uses and definitions. It does not need examples for an unused Process role. A profile using a numerical Property and an aggregate triggers V100-020 even when its label is score rather than Property. Applicability follows meaning and use, not convenient field spelling.

\subsection{Results and mandatory schedule}
Fulfilled means that the required evidence was examined and met the check within scope. Not fulfilled means examined evidence demonstrates a failure. Not assessed means necessary evidence, access or method was unavailable or the check was not performed. Not applicable means the triggering feature is absent, with a documented reason. Lack of access is not non-applicability.

\req{V100-024}{The assessment shall follow Annex A and retain a result, evidence locator, assessor and reason for every requirement. A failed or unassessed applicable requirement shall prevent an unqualified conformity claim for the declared application. Each not-applicable result shall identify the absent trigger.}

\req{V100-026}{Where one requirement groups obligations whose results can differ materially, the assessment shall retain a result and evidence for each such component. The overall requirement shall be fulfilled only when every applicable component is fulfilled. A failed or unassessed component shall remain visible in the requirement-level and overall application result.}

A vocabulary result does not establish factual truth, philosophical exhaustiveness, effective institutional participation or legal validity. Syntactic checking can assist assessment, but cannot determine whether a term is coherently applied to a contested case. The report distinguishes automatic checks from substantive examination of meaning.

\subsection{Maintenance of an assessment}
A change in definition, scope, role criterion, correspondence or assessment-relevant use changes the assessed subject. The application identifies the affected edition and results requiring review. Correcting spelling alone need not change semantics; an unchanged label does not prove semantic stability.

An implementation can retain multiple vocabulary editions for historical records. Assessment identifies which meaning governed each record and which mappings support a combined view. A later interpretation does not retrospectively give earlier actors access to the newer vocabulary. If the historical definition cannot be recovered, that is a reconstruction limitation rather than permission to silently substitute the current one.

\subsection{Execution and evidence record}
The assessor first identifies the application and term-use register, then the feature-triggered requirements. For an applicable check, the evidence includes the definition or dated reference and an actual use. A boundary case is included when needed to distinguish alternative meanings. A documented equivalent case is acceptable when it tests the same distinction in the application's domain. An empty template or undocumented expert understanding is insufficient evidence of actual vocabulary use.

The register records subject version, requirement identifier, applicability, evidence locator, observed result, assessor, date and unresolved limitation. Component results follow V100-026. One evidence item can support several requirements, but their results remain separate. Assessor disagreement identifies whether it concerns meaning, factual material, scope or the assessment method. Restricted evidence receives a scope statement; unavailable required examination evidence receives a not-assessed result rather than an inferred pass. Assessment of an allowed unknown-context declaration follows V100-025 and remains separate from assessment of the unresolved fact.


\GSPannex{informative}{Worked vocabulary cases}
\subsection{Initial distinction cases}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A clerk records that a request arrived late. An attributed entry record can support a reported recording time, subject to the relevant clock, integrity and acquisition-context checks. Whether the request itself arrived late requires identifying the receipt-time source, applicable deadline and inference. The record, target event and deadline assessment remain distinguishable.
\item A later witness report changes the assessment of the arrival time. That is a revision of the account. A later extension of the deadline changes a governing condition. A new definition of ``arrival'' changes the descriptive or institutional rule. One dossier can contain all three histories without conflating them.
\item Two administrations reuse an office name. An application follows their records under the same catalogue heading while recording replacement and disputed institutional continuity. Shared cataloguing does not decide Entity identity.
\item A new procedure reduces review time and makes some participants unable to contest an error. A generativity record can preserve both the increased processing capacity and the restricted capacity for correction; an ethical judgment states its own affected parties and criteria.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{One agreement and several roles}
A fictional agreement links a cooperative and a laboratory. It is a Relation for questions of commitments and access and is tracked as an Entity for continuity after personnel changes. Negotiation and amendment form a Process, signing is an Event, operative terms on a date form a State description and a reported access limit is a Property assignment. These descriptions need not compete for a single exclusive class.

Renewal creates a boundary question. The parties and title continue while terms change substantially. Participants can describe the same agreement, an archive can retain a new document and a legal analyst can ask a separate continuity question. The application retains the criteria and their grounds rather than deriving a universal identity resolution from the shared title. A technical edge that records only an active connection can support a current-status query while losing formation and dispute history.

\subsection{Changing composition in an aggregate}
A programme reports participation rates for two groups. Both groups improve in a later period, while the population contains a larger share of the initially lower-rate group. The pooled rate can decline despite each group's improvement. This is a stipulated comparison problem, not evidence about a real programme.

The Property description retains population, denominator, definitions, weights and periods. A question about the same people over time additionally needs identity linkage. A question about institutional composition can instead treat entry and exit as part of the process. The scalar alone does not establish which question is relevant or whether the outcome is fair.

\subsection{Missing, withheld and inapplicable}
One form records a zero reading, another says no measurement was performed, another withholds an existing value, and another concerns a subject for which the characteristic is inapplicable. A conversion maps every case to an empty cell. That table can have a limited use, but it cannot claim preservation of the distinctions or support a count of zero readings.

A finer vocabulary can recover an omitted value only where retained or newly obtained evidence supports it. Changing notation does not create missing observations. Authorized examination can use a protected source while a public view remains incomplete. The correspondence records the different routes and resulting limitations.

\subsection{Additional analysis in edition 0.2}
FM-007, sections 10.4--10.10, PDF pages 45--50, distinguishes partial definedness, overlapping classifications, scale interpretation, aggregate behavior and changing composition. V100-019/020 preserve these meanings without importing the formal constructions or equating Attribute with Property. The manuscript's arithmetic examples are stipulated constructions, not empirical validation of this vocabulary.

FM-007, sections 17.1--17.2, PDF pages 78--80, distinguishes within-regime consistency from cross-regime alignment and explains the inability of a coarse description to recover omitted distinctions by relabeling. V100-015/018 preserve correspondence limits without claiming that a fixed regime catalogue anticipates every future distinction.

FM-005, sections 5.4.4--5.4.8, PDF pages 32--35, distinguishes observation, inference, latency, hiddenness and negative evidential warrant. V100-016 distinguishes a target's claimed actuality from its inferential accessibility. Missing evidence is not proof of concealment, and inference is not a weaker kind of reality. Sections 5.18.1--5.18.5, pages 152--156, examine visibility, classification power and institutional legibility. The extension requirements make classifications inspectable without assuming that maximal visibility is desirable.

The provisional term Generativity return identifies a programme recording topic, not an asserted equivalence with a manuscript-defined quantity. FM-005, section 5.17.3, PDF page 145, discusses uncertainty in recirculation claims, including conversion or transfer, return pathways and effects on later generative conditions. That passage supports questions to record, while the terminology's wider definition, measurement and evaluative interpretation remain unresolved.
\GSPannex{informative}{Unresolved issues and development evidence}
\subsection{General}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{OPEN --- Property/Attribute.} Compare FM-007 with sustained programme usage and test conflicting value assignments, undefined values, and nominal scales before deciding equivalence, separation, or a terminology change.
\item \textbf{OPEN --- Claim representation.} Compare independently identified claims, attributed assertions within records, and hybrids using disagreement, revision, restricted access, and round-trip examples. This draft requires distinguishability, not a seventh ontology class.
\item \textbf{OPEN --- Role overlap and profiles.} Test overlapping Event/Process and Entity/Relation descriptions. No exclusive schema typing or full cross-role relation vocabulary has been adopted.
\item \textbf{OPEN --- Identity.} Domain-specific criteria and disputed linkage need examples beyond names and administrative identifiers; branching continuity does not have a universal resolution.
\item \textbf{OPEN --- Generativity and return.} Test possibility, foreclosure, contribution, and return pathways without presuming a universal metric or a normative entitlement.
\item \textbf{OPEN --- Source chronology.} Chapter 3 editorial precedence remains unresolved. This draft cites particular passages without declaring one variant authoritative over the other.
\end{itemize}
