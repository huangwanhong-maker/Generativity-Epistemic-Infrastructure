# Programme Brief
## Why, What, and How We Develop the Generativity Standards

**Status:** Preliminary programme brief  
**Version:** 0.4  
**Document class:** Informative programme-level background

## 1. Purpose of this brief

This document gives a concise orientation to the Generativity Standards Program.

It answers three questions:

1. **Why** is a standards programme needed?
2. **What** is being standardized?
3. **How** will the standards be developed, reviewed, published, and maintained?

It is intended to be the first document read by new contributors, reviewers, collaborating organizations, and AI agents before they enter the more detailed governance, theoretical, or technical documents.

---

## 2. Why

### 2.0 Symbolic crisis, recognition, and practical action

The programme's organizing motivation is the symbolic crisis: instability in the connections among symbolic representations, their referents, institutional classifications, and practical trust. GRE Chapter 1, section 1.14, develops this diagnosis. It includes fabricated representations as well as sincere but outdated, partial, or inadequate categories. It does not imply that every representation has become unreliable.

The programme develops epistemic and practical infrastructure through which people and institutions can recognize reality, investigate factual claims, examine their grounds, contest interpretations, revise judgments and action, and preserve the trajectories through which knowledge and future possibilities develop. Archival loss and AI provenance are important components of this broader problem.

Reality, manifestation, representation, evidential warrant, institutional acceptance, and normative legitimacy remain distinguishable. Reality can precede recognition; records and classifications can also enter social relations and change what happens later. Technical conformance, authentic provenance, or institutional acceptance does not by itself establish that a substantive claim is true.

The infrastructure consequently concerns both what changes and how it becomes knowable and actionable. It also concerns who can produce evidence, challenge a classification, obtain review, and participate in shaping the conditions of future generation.

Source interpretation and proposed operational translations are recorded in the [foundation traceability map](docs/planning/foundation_traceability.md). The current development proposal is the [comprehensive plan](docs/planning/comprehensive_development_plan.md).

### 2.1 The historical problem

Contemporary knowledge production increasingly leaves behind abundant outputs while preserving much less information about how those outputs became possible.

A paper may survive while the following disappear:

- the encounters that redirected the inquiry;
- the relations that enabled the work;
- the alternatives that were considered and abandoned;
- the changes in judgment that occurred over time;
- the conditions under which an idea became recognizable;
- the capacities that were created, weakened, or lost;
- the role of human, institutional, computational, and environmental contributors.

A final artifact therefore often preserves substantially less than the generative history from which it emerged.

### 2.2 The AI-era problem

AI-assisted work intensifies this problem.

A simple statement such as “AI was used” does not reveal:

- what role the AI system played;
- which materials were supplied to it;
- which outputs were selected or rejected;
- how human judgment interacted with machine-generated material;
- which later ideas depended on earlier AI-mediated encounters;
- how the trajectory changed through repeated interaction.

As automated and agentic systems become increasingly involved in research, education, policy, design, and administration, conventional provenance records may be too coarse to preserve the relevant history of generation.

### 2.3 The generativity problem

The programme is motivated by a broader GR concern: the history of a process includes more than its manifested outputs.

Of particular interest are changes in:

- available distinctions;
- relational structures;
- constraints;
- possible continuations;
- interpretive frames;
- generative capacities.

A process may produce a modest output while opening an important future trajectory. Another may produce a highly valued output while exhausting the conditions that made future generation possible.

For this reason, output history and generativity history should not be treated as equivalent.

### 2.4 The archival problem

Future observers may need to do more than retrieve an artifact.

They may need to ask:

- How did this become possible?
- Which relations mattered?
- What was known or believed at the time?
- Which alternatives were visible then?
- Which later interpretations rewrote the earlier history?
- Which possibilities remained unrealized?
- Which records are absent, restricted, lost, or destroyed?

A durable standards programme can help preserve enough structured information for later reconstruction, reinterpretation, contestation, and continuation.

## 3. What

### 3.1 Object of standardization

The programme develops standards for the recording, preservation, exchange, presentation, assessment, contestation, and revision of **generativity-related records**, and for their accountable use in inquiry and practical action.

The programme does not attempt to standardize a complete metaphysics of reality.

It develops an operational framework through which generative histories and the grounds of claims about them can be recorded in a consistent, inspectable, and interoperable form. It distinguishes changes in the situation under inquiry, changes in evidence and judgment, and changes in categories or methods of recognition.

### 3.2 Conceptual architecture

Within the wider multilayer architecture, a foundational distinction separates the following two levels.

#### Deep metamodel

The GR background emphasizes four highly abstract dimensions:

- Constraint
- Change
- Structure
- Difference

These belong to the foundational theoretical layer.

#### Operational record ontology

The standards programme currently explores six operational record classes:

- Entity
- State
- Event
- Process
- Relation
- Property

These are candidate standardized forms for recording.

They are not assumed to be ultimate ontological kinds.

### 3.3 What a generativity record may need to preserve

Depending on the profile and use case, a record may include:

- entities and participants;
- states and state changes;
- events and processes;
- relations;
- properties and observations;
- temporal information;
- provenance;
- perspective;
- epistemic status and uncertainty;
- generative conditions;
- contingent encounters;
- decisions and judgments;
- alternatives and unrealized branches;
- enabling and constraining relations;
- later reinterpretations;
- changes in available distinctions;
- changes in future possibilities;
- generativity return;
- access, privacy, restriction, and retention metadata.

### 3.4 Standards family

The programme is intended to produce a family of coordinated documents rather than one monolithic standard.

The provisional family includes:

- **GR-100 — Foundations and Vocabulary**
- **GR-110 — Generativity Recording Requirements**
- **GR-120 — Generativity Information Model**
- **GR-130 — Generativity Interchange Specification**
- **GR-140 — Generativity Presentation Requirements**
- **GR-150 — Conformance and Validation**
- **GR-160 — Preservation, Privacy, Access, and Retention**
- **GR-200 — Epistemic Recording Framework**
- **GR-210 — Evidence, Interpretation, and Contestation**
- **GR-300 — Jurisprudential Interface**
- **GR-310 — Rights, Duties, and Procedural Profiles**
- **GR-400 — Governance and Institutional Use**

These identifiers currently name preliminary standards-track projects, not adopted Standards. Their proposed coordinated scopes and dependencies appear in the comprehensive plan. Existing GR-120/130 project titles are retained; their requirements are distinguished from the implementable details in GR-SPEC-120/130. GR-SPEC-200 supports epistemic representation where draft requirements establish the need.

Supporting publications include:

- manuals;
- implementation guides;
- technical reports;
- schemas;
- validation artifacts;
- worked examples.

### 3.5 Normative and non-normative layers

The programme deliberately separates:

- **normative standards**, which define requirements, recommendations, permissions, and conformance;
- **manuals**, which provide practical guidance;
- **technical reports**, which develop theory, mathematical semantics, comparison, and experimental ideas;
- **foundation manuscripts**, which preserve GR conceptual history and source material.

This separation allows the standards to remain operational without losing contact with their theoretical foundations.

## 3A. Beyond ontology: epistemic, normative, legal, and institutional dimensions

The programme does not assume that a sufficiently detailed ontology alone can produce an adequate generativity record.

A mature framework must also specify how records are:

- known;
- evidenced;
- interpreted;
- contested;
- normatively evaluated;
- legally recognized;
- institutionally used;
- politically and economically situated.

Accordingly, the programme now treats ontology, epistemics, normativity, jurisprudence, governance, political economy, and technical implementation as interacting but distinguishable layers.

See `docs/framework/multilayer_architecture.md`.

## 3B. Dual outputs: standards and policy proposals

The programme may produce two coordinated families of public outputs:

1. **standards and technical infrastructure**, concerned with interoperable requirements, models, profiles, and conformance; and
2. **policy and institutional proposals**, concerned with how international organizations and other institutions may pilot, adopt, govern, or build capacity around these infrastructures.

Policy proposals are not themselves standards, and standards do not by themselves settle political or legal choices.

## 3C. Publication classes

The programme distinguishes three principal public-output families:

1. **Specifications** — technically precise and implementable documents that may be experimental, candidate, or stable without yet being formal Standards.
2. **Standards** — formally adopted normative publications with programme-level conformance authority.
3. **International Political / Policy Proposals** — recommendatory institutional or policy publications directed to international organizations or other transnational actors.

This distinction prevents technical precision, formal standardization, and political/institutional recommendation from being treated as the same kind of authority.

See `docs/framework/publication_taxonomy.md`.

## 4. How

### Protocols within publication classes

A protocol describes behavior: participants, interactions, states, transitions, outcomes and failures. Protocols are technical subjects documented in Specifications or, after formal adoption, Standards. They do not create a separate publication authority class. Institutional review procedures and machine-to-machine protocols can support the same purpose while requiring different implementation and assessment methods.

### Comparative and cross-domain development

The programme studies ISO/IEC and IEEE standards-development approaches, ITU technical publication practice, and relevant UN-system quality and institutional frameworks to inform its own proportionate procedures. The [comparative study](docs/planning/standards_development_comparison.md) distinguishes source practices, proposed adaptations and unresolved governance choices. The [drafting-model study](docs/references/drafting_models.md) records verified ISO, IEEE, ITU and UN sources, their different functions, and access limitations. This work does not imply affiliation, accreditation or compliance with those organizations' procedures.

The [cross-domain programme](docs/planning/cross_domain_programme.md) connects the mission to epistemology, archival practice, jurisprudence, ethics, institutional governance, public policy, political economy and implementation. These domains contribute substantive work and review throughout development; they are not reduced to metadata fields or a final compliance check.


### 4A. Standards-first development

The default programme logic begins with the Standard.

The Standard identifies what a conforming practice should achieve. If those requirements create a need for shared technical infrastructure, the programme develops one or more Specifications.

This yields a recurring development loop:

Standard Working Draft
→ Specification need
→ Specification
→ pilot
→ revision of the Standard

The Standard and Specification may therefore co-evolve before publication.


### 4.1 Develop the standards programme before the standards

The programme first establishes the rules by which standards are created.

The initial governance layer includes:

- Standards Programme Charter;
- Directives Part 1 — Procedures for Development and Maintenance;
- Directives Part 2 — Structure and Drafting Rules;
- Technical Committee Charter;
- public review procedure;
- consensus and decision policy;
- conflict-of-interest and ethics policy;
- intellectual property policy;
- maintenance and versioning procedure;
- records and transparency policy.

This creates a defined process through which later GR standards can claim procedural consistency.

Expanded preparatory version 0.2 texts for this governance layer and GR-100/110/200/210 are available in the [working-draft package](publications/README.md), with formal document structure, detailed requirements and assessment schedules. Version 0.1 is preserved in a [source archive](publications/archive/0.1/README.md). Drafting and compilation do not complete governance adoption or authorize formal technical project initiation. The [expanded review record](docs/reviews/expanded_working_draft_review.md) distinguishes completed authoring from remaining institutional and pilot evidence.

### 4.2 Development lifecycle

The provisional standards lifecycle is:

| Stage | Status |
|---|---|
| 00 | Preliminary |
| 10 | Proposal |
| 20 | Working Draft |
| 30 | Committee Draft |
| 40 | Public Review Draft |
| 50 | Final Approval Draft |
| 60 | Published Standard |
| 90 | Review / Revision / Withdrawal |

The process is intended to remain lighter than ISO while preserving comparable principles of traceability, review, versioning, and maintenance.

### 4.3 Evidence and source discipline

The programme distinguishes several kinds of authority.

- Governance directives govern the standards-development process.
- Published standards govern conformance.
- Accepted ADRs govern project architecture.
- Foundation manuscripts provide source authority for GR concepts.
- Working drafts and planning documents remain provisional.

Agents and contributors should not silently convert a theoretical statement into a normative requirement.

Where a standard intentionally narrows or operationalizes a broader GR concept, the divergence should be explicit and traceable.

### 4.4 Foundation manuscript workflow

The folder `foundation_manuscripts/` serves as the project-local GR source corpus.

When relevant manuscripts are added, contributors should:

1. index them in `foundation_manuscripts/MANIFEST.md`;
2. identify important concepts and notation;
3. record historical changes and tensions;
4. map relevant ideas to candidate standards clauses;
5. preserve disagreements and revisions;
6. create an ADR when a standards-specific interpretation materially departs from the source corpus.

### 4.5 Issue and decision traceability

Substantive changes should leave a visible record.

The preferred pattern is:

\[
\text{Issue}
\rightarrow
\text{Alternatives}
\rightarrow
\text{Decision}
\rightarrow
\text{Rationale}
\rightarrow
\text{Affected documents}
\]

Architecture-level decisions are recorded as ADRs.

Public-review comments should likewise record:

\[
\text{Comment}
\rightarrow
\text{Disposition}
\rightarrow
\text{Rationale}
\rightarrow
\text{Resulting change}
\]

The development process should itself exemplify the kind of generative traceability the standards seek to support.

### 4.6 Incremental implementation

The programme should avoid attempting to solve the entire conceptual problem before implementation begins.

A preferred development strategy is:

1. establish governance;
2. stabilize core vocabulary;
3. define a minimal coordinated recording, epistemic, presentation, protection and review profile;
4. test it first on bounded examples and then on appropriately governed real use cases;
5. develop the information model;
6. build exchange schemas;
7. test independent implementations;
8. revise through public and technical review;
9. refine conformance using assessment methods drafted alongside the requirements;
10. maintain the standards as the field evolves.

### 4.7 Pilot-driven development

Candidate pilots include:

- individual research;
- AI-assisted manuscript development;
- collaborative research;
- institutional decision processes;
- historical reconstruction from incomplete archives.

Pilots are used to test whether the standards are sufficiently expressive without becoming impractically burdensome.

The current planning proposal begins with the programme's own documented decision history and a synthetic contested institutional case. A complete pilot cycle includes evidence, interpretation, challenge, reasoned response or revision, protection, presentation and practical consequences. Real external pilots depend on actual institutional participation and appropriate access arrangements. No participation is presumed.

The [comprehensive development plan](docs/planning/comprehensive_development_plan.md) sets out the proposed portfolio, milestone evidence, dependencies and immediate work queue. The [earlier preliminary plan](docs/planning/preliminary_plan.md) remains available as a historical draft. Planning does not substitute for governance adoption or work-item approval.

## 5. Design principles

The programme currently follows these principles:

- preserve trajectory, not only output;
- distinguish occurrence from interpretation;
- distinguish present validity from historical existence;
- treat relations as first-class when their own histories matter;
- preserve uncertainty rather than forcing premature certainty;
- preserve alternatives without implying equal feasibility;
- separate preservation from publicity;
- expose mediation in reconstructed presentations;
- distinguish evidence integrity from the warrant of a claim;
- support meaningful contestation and revision of action as well as correction of records;
- examine how recording and recognition affect participation and future possibilities;
- support future reinterpretation;
- maintain compatibility with GR foundations without turning philosophical theory directly into compliance language;
- allow standards to evolve while preserving their own development history.

## 6. Intended outcome

The long-term goal is a standards infrastructure that supports warranted, revisable recognition and practical action while enabling future actors to recover more than a final artifact.

A successful record should help a later observer understand, within the limits of available evidence:

- what changed;
- what relations mattered;
- what was recognized at the time;
- what alternatives were available;
- how judgments evolved;
- how the field of future possibilities changed;
- and how the recorded process may be continued or reinterpreted.

The programme therefore aims at three linked capacities:

\[
\text{future re-recognizability}
+
\text{reinterpretability}
+
\text{regenerability}.
\]

These capacities provide the broad orientation for the standards family while remaining subject to refinement through the formal standards-development process.
