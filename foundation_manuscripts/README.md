# Foundation Manuscripts

This folder is the project-local source corpus for Generative Relational (GR) theory and related foundational work.

## Purpose

Foundation manuscripts are used to:

- preserve the conceptual history of GR;
- provide source-grounded definitions, distinctions, notation, and examples;
- help agents maintain consistency across standards, manuals, technical reports, and schemas;
- expose tensions, revisions, and unresolved differences between manuscripts rather than silently smoothing them away.

## What belongs here

Copy foundational GR works into this folder, including, where relevant:

- metamodel papers;
- ontology and relation papers;
- generativity / generativity-return works;
- trust, criticality, and relational-reality works;
- historiographical justice and archive-related works;
- political-philosophy foundations;
- theoretical notes that materially define GR terminology.

PDF, Markdown, TeX, and plain-text sources are all acceptable.

## Source status

Foundation manuscripts are **conceptual source material**, not automatically normative standards.

A statement in a manuscript does not become a standards requirement merely because it appears here.

When developing a standard:

1. consult relevant foundation manuscripts;
2. preserve terminology and distinctions where feasible;
3. identify conflicts or historical changes;
4. record deliberate departures;
5. translate theory into normative language only through an explicit standards decision.

## Precedence

For this repository, use the following precedence when conflicts occur:

1. applicable governance directives for process;
2. published normative standards for conformance;
3. accepted architecture decision records (ADRs);
4. foundation manuscripts for GR conceptual interpretation;
5. active working drafts and planning notes;
6. informal examples and exploratory material.

A later standard may intentionally define a narrower operational term than a foundation manuscript. Such divergence should be documented rather than hidden.

## Indexing

After adding manuscripts, update `MANIFEST.md`.

For each manuscript, record:

- filename;
- title;
- date/version;
- status;
- principal concepts;
- notation;
- relevant standards;
- known tensions with other manuscripts;
- whether later work revises it.

## Consistency rule

Agents should never infer that two manuscript formulations are equivalent merely because they sound similar. Preserve differences until an explicit decision resolves them.
