**Document class:** Informative source register

## 1. Scope and source handling

This register identifies the project-local foundation corpus. FM identifiers identify particular files, not an adopted doctrine or a claim of publication precedence. Both Chapter 3 files remain separately identifiable. No PDF has been renamed or replaced.

Titles below come from the opening pages. Author attribution for the GRE chapters comes from their PDF metadata; the two metamodel papers name the author on their opening pages. Publication dates and formal publication status have not been verified. PDF creation timestamps are build metadata, not evidence of publication or approval. A filename containing `first_draft` is identified as a filename label rather than treated as independently verified editorial status.

Section references and page numbers refer to the supplied PDF, using one-based PDF pages. These entries provide a planning-oriented source map, not a complete collation, mathematical review, or verification of the manuscripts' external references. An external work cited inside a manuscript has not thereby been independently verified for use in a programme publication.

The companion [foundation traceability map](../docs/planning/foundation_traceability.md) connects passages to candidate requirements, unresolved questions, and prospective pilot evidence. Those operational translations remain proposals.

### FM-004 — The Ontological View of Generative Relational Theory

- **File:** [GRE_Chapter_04.pdf](GRE_Chapter_04.pdf), 183 pages.
- **Author:** Wanhong Huang, as recorded in PDF metadata.
- **Date:** Publication/manuscript date unknown.
- **Version/status:** Chapter 4; formal publication/revision status unknown.
- **Primary concepts:** Ontological, relational, and manifestational reality; ontological suspension; manifestation relations; non-invertibility; symbolization; recursive effects of representation; scale and historical categories.
- **Important passages:** Three registers (§4.2, pp. 11–19); ontological suspension (§4.3, pp. 19–27); manifestation and its mappings (§§4.6–4.9, pp. 43–74); symbolization and recursion (§§4.10–4.11, pp. 74–89); reality, access, and epistemic reconstruction (§4.22, pp. 172–180).
- **Terminology and notation:** `M_C: X ⇝ Y` (Equation 4.1, p. 65) denotes a general manifestation mapping under conditions `C`. Deterministic, stochastic, partial, multivalued, and historically variable realizations remain open. It is not a universal single-valued function or proposed interchange syntax.
- **Relevant programme documents:** GR-100/110/120/140/200; epistemic architecture; formal-semantics technical reports. Its treatment of institutional reality informs questions for GR-300/400 without establishing legal effect.
- **Supersedes / revised by:** No verified supersession; conceptually informs FM-005's epistemology.
- **Known tensions or ambiguities:** The three registers answer different analytical questions; they are not three substances, exclusive object bins, or an obligatory temporal pipeline. Relational reality can precede recognition, while a manifestation can later alter relational reality. Ontological suspension preserves weaker warranted claims and does not entail denial.
- **Notes for contributors:** Do not implement the registers as mutually exclusive record classes without a separately argued operational decision. Distinguish a target, its manifestation, a record of that manifestation, and a claim about it.

- **File:** [GRE_Chapter_05_Complete_First_Draft.pdf](GRE_Chapter_05_Complete_First_Draft.pdf), 196 pages.
- **Author:** Wanhong Huang, as recorded in PDF metadata.
- **Date:** Publication/manuscript date unknown.
- **Version/status:** “Complete First Draft” is the filename label; independent editorial/publication status unknown.
- **Primary concepts:** Mediated access; inverse problems; trace and evidence; performative representation; partial observation; boundary and scale; identity; generative attribution; historical reconstruction; uncertainty; epistemic justice; machine mediation; revisability.
- **Important passages:** Inverse problem (§5.2, pp. 13–20); trace/evidence (§5.4, pp. 28–36); performative representation (§5.6, pp. 44–53); generative attribution and trajectories (§§5.13–5.14, pp. 105–122); evidence and uncertainty (§§5.15–5.17, pp. 122–150); epistemic justice (§5.18, pp. 150–160); machine mediation (§5.19, pp. 160–168); revisability (§5.20, pp. 168–176).
- **Terminology and notation:** `K(y | C, E, A)` (Equation 5.1, p. 14) is an epistemically admissible compatibility set given manifestation `y`, conditions `C`, evidence `E`, and auxiliary assumptions `A`. Compatibility is not assumed to be numerical or probabilistic. Definitions 5.159–5.167 distinguish revisability, provisional maps, evidence updates, intervention-sensitive revision, historical revision, and category revision.
- **Relevant programme documents:** GR-100/110/120/140/150/160/200/210; claim/evidence, contestation, revision, and institutional praxis work in GR-400. Procedural questions inform GR-310 only with a separately identified institutional or legal basis.
- **Supersedes / revised by:** No verified supersession. Develops epistemic questions following FM-004 without replacing its distinctions.
- **Known tensions or ambiguities:** Manifestation does not uniquely identify its source; availability of a record does not establish warrant. Changes in the target world, evidence, and descriptive categories require distinguishable histories. Epistemic justice retains evidential standards while examining unequal conditions for producing, preserving, interpreting, and contesting evidence.
- **Notes for contributors:** Operational claim types and metadata are proposals to test against these distinctions, not adopted definitions of knowledge or truth. Preserve alternative explanations and the difference between discovery and intervention-produced change.

- **Date:** Publication/manuscript date unknown.

- **Important passages:** Scope (p. 2); precise base assumptions (§3, pp. 10–16); six categories (§§7–12, pp. 26–61); coordination (§15, pp. 71–75); philosophical scope (§16, pp. 75–78); regime changes (§17, pp. 78–83); prospective research design (§19, pp. 87–90).
- **Terminology and notation:** Entity, Relation, Event, Attribute, State, Process. Distinct equivalence constructions have different roles; use of the same mathematical structure does not equate them.
- **Relevant programme documents:** GR-100/110/120/150/200/210; identity, event, process, missingness, migration, and formal-semantics work; pilot design.
- **Supersedes / revised by:** Continues FM-006 with explicit assumptions and enrichments. Does not establish exhaustive formalization of emergence or empirical validity of every construction.

- **Notes for contributors:** Use the counterexamples and prospective research design to identify losses in a recording proposal. The finite examples are declared constructions, not completed empirical pilots. Record a decision before translating Attribute into Property or turning selected analytical roles into exclusive schema types.

Programme-level disposition belongs in [open questions](../docs/planning/open_questions.md) and, where a design choice changes architecture, a decision record. This register preserves source differences; it does not resolve them by editorial harmonization.
