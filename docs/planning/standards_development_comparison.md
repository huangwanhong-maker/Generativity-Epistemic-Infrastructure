**Document class:** Informative comparative planning analysis

## 1. Purpose and limits

**Role:** Compare established standards-development mechanisms and identify what this programme has reason and capacity to adopt, adapt, or defer.

The programme's motivation is epistemic and practical: make the grounds of recognition, judgment, classification, contestation, and action inspectable while preserving consequential generative trajectories. Its procedures consequently matter as much as the eventual record model. An inaccessible or unchallengeable development process would undermine the practice the programme seeks to support.

ISO/IEC and IEEE SA provide established approaches to initiating work, preparing drafts, consulting affected interests, handling disagreement, approving publications, and maintaining them. Their organizational structures, constituencies, resources, and authority differ. This comparison does not identify one universal development procedure or propose copying either institution's committees into a small programme.

The labels **ADOPT**, **ADAPT**, and **DEFER** below describe proposed directions for future decisions. None establishes a rule in this document. ISO, IEC, IEEE, and WTO references indicate sources, not affiliation, recognition, accreditation, permission to use institutional marks, or a claim of formal compliance. This analysis does not determine legal obligations or provide legal advice.

## 3. Comparison and proposed tailoring

**Role:** Separate observed mechanisms from proposed GR programme choices. The right-hand column is new planning analysis, not a statement that the cited organizations prescribe it for this programme.

## 4. Development stages: useful correspondence, distinct authority

**Role:** Relate the programme's existing lifecycle to established practice without relabelling it as an ISO or IEEE process.

ISO's published stage scheme includes proposal, preparatory, committee, enquiry, approval, publication, review, and withdrawal stages. IEEE's explanatory lifecycle covers initiation, group formation, drafting, balloting, final approval, and maintenance. [ISO stage codes](https://www.iso.org/stage-codes.html); [IEEE quick reference](https://standards.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/import/documents/other/ieee_sa_toolkit.pdf)

| Programme work | Useful comparison | Programme-specific consequence |
|---|---|---|
| M0/M1: orientation and governance | Institutional procedures precede an authoritative development decision. | Establish founding authority, participation, review, and appeal capacity before formal project approval. |
| Stages 00/10 and scoped NWIP | Preliminary study and explicit project initiation | Authorize a bounded normative problem; source analysis and exploratory examples remain distinguishable from approved work. |
| Stages 20/30; M2–M4 | Expert drafting and wider technical consideration | Co-develop requirements and only the Specifications/protocols needed to test them; preserve unresolved theoretical differences. |
| Stages 40/50; M5 | Wider review and a recorded approval decision | Public review, horizontal findings, objections, remedies, and maintenance evidence inform adoption. A completed schema does not bypass these steps. |
| Stage 60; M6 and stage 90 | Publication and subsequent maintenance | Declare edition and scope; maintain issues, compatibility, correction, and withdrawal records. |

The programme currently groups review/revision/withdrawal under stage 90. ISO's published scheme distinguishes stage 95 for withdrawal. This is an explicit tailoring difference, not a reason to renumber repository documents. Local stages and M0–M6 milestones describe different things: document status and programme work respectively.

## 5. Participation models to decide

**Role:** Identify institutional options and their political-economy consequences before selecting voting rules. The contrast between ISO's member-mediated participation and IEEE's individual/entity methods motivates comparison; the options below are programme proposals. [ISO development overview](https://www.iso.org/developing-standards.html); [IEEE Bylaws, 5.2.1](https://standards.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/import/documents/other/sb_bylaws.pdf)

| Option | Potential fit | Risk and proposed safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Individual contributors acting in their own capacity | Accessible launch; direct epistemic, practical, and affected-person input without needing institutional sponsorship | Professional networks and available unpaid time can dominate. Record affiliations and participation gaps; use asynchronous review, accessible materials, and declared eligibility for decision roles. One employer's several contributors do not automatically demonstrate stakeholder diversity. |
| Institutional membership and designated representatives | Clear institutional mandates, sustained contributions, and accountable organizational commitments | Large funders or implementers can crowd out affected people and smaller organizations. Distinguish representation from expertise, manage related entities and conflicts, and provide non-member comment and challenge routes. Capacity to administer membership is currently unproven. |
| Hybrid participation with explicit decision roles | Combines individuals, affected communities, and organizational mandates | Double counting and ambiguous authority are possible. State which capacity each person uses for each decision, separate consultation from formal decision rights, and justify balance arrangements. Avoid creating multiple chambers before participants and administration exist. |

The **PROVISIONAL planning preference** is an open individual-contributor launch with affiliation disclosure, deliberate stakeholder outreach, bounded delegated decision roles, and neutral procedural review. Institutional representatives can contribute with their capacity clearly identified. Transition to institutional or hybrid membership remains a later charter decision. This preference does not grant membership or voting rights now.

A participant register records the role and basis of participation, relevant affiliation, declared conflicts, and review competence, with a protected route for information that cannot safely be public. Representation claims need evidence of a mandate or consultation; demographic or geographic breadth alone does not establish that a person speaks for others.

## 6. Neutral review during bootstrap

**Role:** Make challenge and remedy possible with a small team while preserving the distinction between procedural authority and substantive truth.

The proposed launch arrangement identifies an editor/coordinator, an authorized progression decision role, and at least an available route to a neutral reviewer outside the disputed decision. These are functions to staff, not assumed existing bodies. Neutrality is assessed through involvement, interests, conflicts, and acceptance of the review procedure rather than asserted by job title. IEEE's conflict-sensitive appeal mechanism provides a useful comparison; its institutional panel size and deadlines are not imported. [IEEE Operations Manual, 5.8](https://standards.ieee.org/about/policies/opman/sect5/)

Candidate capacity arrangements are an agreed roster of external volunteer reviewers or a later reciprocal review arrangement with another organization. Neither is presently staffed or agreed. A temporary founding steward can coordinate preliminary drafts, but that role alone cannot provide independent review of its own consequential contested decision. Where impartial consideration is unavailable, the proposed response is to retain draft status or defer the disputed progression decision while continuing unaffected research.

The governance package distinguishes:

- **Technical or conceptual objection:** consideration by the responsible drafting/review group, with source evidence, alternatives, reasons, and recorded remaining disagreement.
- **Procedural appeal:** review of whether the operative process, participation rights, conflicts, and decision authority were respected; available remedies include reconsideration or repeating an affected stage.
- **Ethics or conduct complaint:** a protected route capable of addressing retaliation, harassment, misuse of sensitive records, or domination without making public disclosure a condition of being heard.
- **Record-specific correction or access request:** the route established for the relevant pilot or institution, with the applicable authority and retention conditions identified.
- **Claim requiring an external legal remedy:** identified as outside the programme's adjudicative authority; a contextual review determines the appropriate interface without implying that internal appeal replaces it.

These distinctions prevent one technical chair from becoming a general authority over truth, morality, participation complaints, and legal rights. The relationship between routes, limits on repeated abusive submissions, publication pauses, and available remedies remains governance drafting work.

## 7. Applying the WTO principles proportionately

**Role:** Use the six principles as a diagnostic of participation and relevance. The source is a TBT Committee decision about international standardization; the following operationalization is this programme's proposal. [WTO six principles](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/tbt_e/principles_standards_tbt_e.htm)

| Source principle | Proposed programme question and evidence |
|---|---|
| Transparency | Can a reader locate proposed work, its motivation, draft status, objections, decision reasons, and maintenance owner? Demonstrate the public decision trail and justified restrictions. |
| Openness | Can a relevant person learn of the work and contribute before decisions become fixed? Test contact routes, draft access, meeting alternatives, and the handling of unsolicited relevant comments. |
| Impartiality and consensus | Do funding, affiliation, technical fluency, or control of tools prevent a concern from receiving fair consideration? Review participation, conflicts, and sustained objections as well as votes. |
| Effectiveness and relevance | Does the practice improve inspectability and contestability at a feasible burden? Use the bounded pilots and report failures, exclusions, and circumstances in which standardization adds little value. |
| Coherence | Is an existing standard reusable, and where do semantic or authority boundaries differ? Record source editions, gaps, mappings, and tests before compatibility claims. |
| Development dimension | What resource, language, connectivity, or institutional barriers exclude potential participants? Assess asynchronous options, accessible summaries, translation needs, and support that actual resources can sustain. |

Applying these questions does not establish international representativeness or WTO recognition. Limited participation remains a reported limitation of the evidence supporting adoption.

## 8. Concrete governance-development outputs

**Role:** Translate the comparison into work under the existing plan rather than adding another layer of nominal institutions.

1. **Source/edition verification note:** obtain and review Part 1:2026 and its transition arrangements, Part 2:2021 with Amendment 1:2026, and recheck IEEE policy updates before adopting source-dependent clauses. Preserve the present access limitation and earlier-edition references in the history.
2. **Charter options memorandum:** compare founding authority, individual/institutional/hybrid participation, decision-role appointment and removal, financial influence, and routes to neutral review. Record the selected option and rationale through the programme's governance process.
3. **Directives Part 1 draft:** define initiation, review, consensus assessment, ballots if selected, disposition, appeal and complaint routes, publication, maintenance, and exceptions. Choose periods and thresholds only after the participation and capacity decisions.
4. **Directives Part 2 draft:** operationalize publication classes, source authority, normative language, testability, cross-references, versioning, and conformance boundaries. Keep GR interpretations traceable to foundation sources and operational decisions.
5. **Horizontal-review record:** for each work item, identify the motivation, affected interests, epistemic assumptions, ethical impacts, legal/institutional boundary, public-policy alternatives, burden distribution, technical evidence, and reviewer competence or gaps.
6. **Contribution and publication policy options:** compare rights for documents, code, schemas, examples, and data; obtain qualified review for the actual organizational and jurisdictional context. No license, patent commitment, or third-party entitlement is established by this plan.
7. **Governance rehearsal:** exercise a contested proposal, minority objection, recusal, neutral appeal, material draft change, correction, and withdrawal using synthetic material or the programme's appropriately shareable history. Revise procedures before treating them as coherent launch infrastructure.

Formal ISO/IEC or IEEE participation, adoption through another standards body, external certification, and international-institution endorsement remain **DEFERRED options**. Each would require its own purpose, eligibility and resource assessment, institutional agreement where applicable, and explicit authorization. The present objective is a credible, usable programme process proportionate to its actual community and mission.
