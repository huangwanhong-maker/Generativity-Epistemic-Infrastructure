## Problem statement

People and institutions act through records and representations whose grounding, interpretation and history are often difficult to inspect. Symbolic crisis makes the connections among referents, claims, evidence, classifications and practical trust a central programme concern. Final artifacts also underdescribe the relations, judgments, alternatives and changes in future possibilities through which they emerged.

The initial work item investigates what shared recording and procedural requirements make these connections inspectable and revisable without equating provenance, institutional acceptance, technical validation or consensus with truth.

## Scope

A coordinated, bounded development package for representing a situation, attributing claims, identifying evidence and limitations, tracing relevant generative conditions, presenting competing interpretations, receiving a challenge, recording a reasoned response or revision, and preserving usable history under declared access and retention conditions.

The first case is proposed to be the programme's own documented decision history, paired with a synthetic institutional decision case. Both can expose methodological and implementation problems before real external participants or sensitive institutional data are involved.

## Out of scope for the first package

- A universal metaphysics, truth certification, or numerical score for trust or generativity.
- Exhaustive capture of lived experience or every unrealized possibility.
- Automated adjudication of disputed substantive claims.
- General legal admissibility, jurisdiction-independent rights, or binding policy adoption.
- A mandated platform, distributed ledger, global identity provider or federated network.
- Completion or adoption of every publication in the portfolio.

## Need for standardization

Independent records and implementations need a shared account of what an assertion means, whose assertion it is, how evidence bears on it, which histories and disagreements are preserved, and what an exchange or presentation omits. Common procedural expectations can make review and correction usable across institutions while permitting scoped local decisions and legal profiles.

## Intended users and stakeholders

Initial users include researchers, record custodians, reviewers, implementers, institutional decision makers and people represented or affected by records. Relevant participation includes people with limited recording resources, users needing accessible presentations, epistemic and archival expertise, and protection and procedural expertise. Representation and recruitment arrangements are governance work, not established facts.

## Required expertise and organization

Roles include a coordinating editor, source/terminology reviewer, epistemic reviewer, implementer, archival/protection reviewer, procedural reviewer and affected-user participants. Names and availability are OPEN. The existing GR/TC 1 mandate and working-group structure are candidates for assignment after governance launch; no additional committee is presumed.

## Risks and controls to evaluate

Evaluate misleading truth signals; category reification; forced agreement; erased uncertainty; exposure of sensitive content; burdens on underrepresented participants; malicious or repetitive challenges; and unmaintainable technical complexity. Protection measures are use-case-specific and evaluated alongside usability. Retaining a revision trail does not imply indefinite retention of deleted sensitive content.

## Expected implementation and pilot evidence

Use the initial cycle and proposed gates in the [comprehensive plan](comprehensive_development_plan.md) and [governance and pilot plan](governance_and_pilots.md). Evidence includes a full challenge/revision cycle, preserved disagreement, a change in available evidence or categories, a traced enabling or constraining relation, restricted or absent evidence, and explicit failure handling.

Interoperability maturity requires independently implemented exchange of the declared features. Two datasets processed by one implementation do not demonstrate independent interoperability. A conforming structure does not establish substantive truth or legal validity.
