# Governance launch and pilot evidence plan

**Document class:** Informative programme planning document  
**Status:** PROVISIONAL

**Authority:** Proposed work and evaluation criteria; no governance adoption, project approval, or conformance requirements are established by this document.

## 1. Role and planning basis

**Authoring update, 2026-09-22:** [Substantive governance drafts](../../publications/README.md), expanded templates, [P0 arithmetic/design rehearsal](../../examples/p0-governance-rehearsal.md) and [P1 disputed-record fixture](../../examples/p1-contested-decision.md) are available. The original outline links in the checklist below now point onward to those drafts. Actual appointments, review, participation, adoption and live pilot evidence remain pending; authored cases do not satisfy the operational completion criteria.

This plan connects the programme's governance infrastructure to the evidence needed to develop standards, protocols, and specifications. It accompanies the [programme brief](../../PROGRAMME_BRIEF.md), the [comprehensive development plan](comprehensive_development_plan.md), and the [publication taxonomy](../framework/publication_taxonomy.md).

The planning objective is an inspectable practice of recognizing, recording, contesting, revising, and presenting generative trajectories. Archival preservation and AI-assisted work are important cases within that objective. Recording final outputs alone is insufficient to exercise it.

The [standards-development comparison](standards_development_comparison.md) supplies verified ISO/IEC, IEEE and WTO source practices and proposed adaptations for governance drafting. The [cross-domain programme](cross_domain_programme.md) supplies substantive epistemic, ethical, jurisprudential, institutional, policy and political-economy work and review. Neither is reduced to a technical validation checklist.

Existing publication numbers remain project-family identifiers. References below to GR-100, GR-110, and other families identify preliminary scopes, not adopted Standards or approved projects. Protocol experiments acquire their publication class and authority from their containing Specification, Standard Working Draft, or other declared publication; the word "protocol" does not itself confer authority.

Named roles below are proposed responsibilities. Their holders, availability, institutional mandates, and resources are **UNASSIGNED**. The existing repository does not establish that the proposed Council, committee, or working groups are constituted. Sequencing is based on evidence and capacity, without committed dates, budgets, or external partners.

## 3. Founding sequence and accountable roles

**Role:** Avoid treating draft governance as already operative or using a technical project to manufacture its own approval authority.

### 3.1 Proposed founding sequence

1. **Declare the founding basis.** Record who is proposing the programme, in what capacity, and through which explicit founding decision governance can be adopted. Select a provisional stewardship arrangement or a constituted governing body after examining available participation. Neither arrangement is established by this plan.
2. **Prepare the coherent governance package.** Resolve dependencies across the charter, directives, committee mandate, review/decision/appeal procedures, contribution policies, maintenance, and transparency. Preliminary drafting, source studies, and synthetic rehearsals can proceed during this step without formal standards-project status.
3. **Review the package and rehearse its procedures.** Invite relevant perspectives, document participation gaps, and exercise a proposal, contested comment, conflict recusal, appeal, correction, and withdrawal. Record failures and revisions. Avoid presenting an internal rehearsal as independent public review.
4. **Record explicit founding adoption.** The identified founding participants record the authority, text versions, decision basis, dissent, and remaining limitations. A transitional arrangement includes its scope, limits, review trigger, and replacement route. The adoption record remains separate from an architecture ADR.
5. **Constitute and assign.** Appoint actual accountable holders; publish participation and contact routes; establish a functioning comment, complaint, and appeal route with appropriate confidentiality. Multiple responsibilities can be combined in a small team, with conflicts recorded and independent review arranged for contested decisions.
6. **Approve bounded work items through the operative procedure.** Only after governance coherence is evidenced, consider formal approval of GR-100/GR-110 and coordinated dependent work. A family-wide NWIP can provide context, while each deliverable retains a bounded scope, owner, dependencies, and progression decision.

An unresolved founding-authority question, unusable appeal route, or unfilled approval responsibility keeps formal project/adoption decisions pending. This does not prevent preliminary research and openly labelled experiments. The founding decision is a future governance action, not an outcome claimed by this planning revision.

## 4. Evidence gates

**Role:** Define proposed exit evidence for planning. These are not substitutes for the future adopted directives or automatic promotion rules.

| Gate | Evidence considered for progression | Accountable decision role, once assigned | Consequence of an unresolved material gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| GOV — governance ready | Adopted coherent package; founding record; assigned authorities; usable review/appeal/contact routes; completed procedural rehearsals and dispositions | Founding decision authority, then constituted oversight role | Continue preliminary work; defer formal standards-project approval. |
| SCOPE — work item ready | Defined normative problem, users, conformance subject and exclusions, sources, dependencies, alternatives, affected stakeholders, pilot plan, and feasible ownership | Project approval authority | Revise or defer the proposal. |
| PILOT — experiment ready | Versioned candidate clauses and protocol/specification dependencies; scenario questions; minimal data plan; participants and access authorization when relevant; stop/recovery procedure | Pilot lead, with data-steward clearance for the proposed data | Use synthetic material or defer dependent data collection. |
| REVIEW — draft ready for public review | Integrated pilot evidence; source/requirement/test traceability; negative cases; known limitations; privacy, accessibility, and institutional impacts; disposition of material internal defects | Authorized review/progression authority | Narrow scope or revise; do not conceal failed cases. |
| ADOPT — publication ready | Public review and dispositions; justified treatment of sustained objections; completed technical and horizontal review; precise conformance claims; operative normative dependencies; formal approval under adopted procedure; maintenance ownership | Adoption authority defined by governance | Retain draft or Specification status, or defer adoption. |
| MAINTAIN — release remains supportable | Issue and incident handling; correction/revision history; compatibility evidence; maintenance capacity; reasons for confirmation, revision, supersession, or withdrawal | Maintenance owner and authorized decision authority | Publish limitations and use the adopted maintenance/withdrawal procedure. |

There is no universal pass percentage or fixed pilot count in this plan. Reviewers examine whether the evidence supports the specific claim and whether failures change that claim. Future numerical thresholds need a stated purpose, baseline, and rationale. Passing a pilot is evidence for review, not procedural approval.

## 5. Pilot sequence and first integrated release

**Role:** Exercise the programme's epistemic and practical purpose before multiplying formats or institutional commitments.

The first integrated experimental release combines vocabulary and recording with epistemic representation, generativity-related trajectory, correction/contestation, presentation, and protection. It draws on the preliminary scopes of GR-100/110/140/150/160/200/210; GR-120 and technical Specifications supply infrastructure only where candidate requirements need it. GR-300/310/400 inform bounded institutional and procedural questions without asserting legal authority.

| Pilot | Proposed material and question | Distinctive evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| P0 — programme decision history | The programme's own publicly shareable proposal, alternatives, objection, source interpretation, decision, and later revision. Can another reader reconstruct what was known and decided at each point? | Links between issues, drafts, reasons, and changed scope; conflicting accounts preserved with attribution; restrictions applied to contributor information. Useful as a self-pilot, but its authors' familiarity limits independent usability evidence. A rehearsal before GOV does not imply a formal standards decision. |
| P1 — contested institutional decision | Synthetic allocation or eligibility decision involving conflicting accounts, incomplete evidence, disputed identity, and a revised classification. Can a participant challenge the representation and understand the resulting revision? | Separates occurrence claims, evidence, institutional acceptance, normative evaluation, and claimed legal status; demonstrates reply, correction, review, and differentiated access. Synthetic ground truth is confined to the scenario and does not establish a general truth-detection method. |
| P2 — AI-assisted research trajectory | A bounded research task with human and AI contributions, source checking, rejected suggestions, a change of judgment, and an enabling encounter or constraint. | Records consequential contributions and decisions, uncertainty about system behavior, and branches relevant to the research. No demand for inaccessible internal model reasoning or exhaustive interaction capture. AI output and detailed provenance do not establish factual correctness. |
| P3 — incomplete archive | Licensed/public or synthetic fragments with conflicting dates, missing material, retrospective interpretation, and uncertain attribution. | An account that remains useful while explicitly distinguishing surviving evidence, inference, unknown chronology, restricted access, loss, and retrospective reconstruction. It does not fill gaps with invented events or probabilities. |
| P4 — consenting external institution | A bounded workflow selected later with a willing partner, participants, appropriate access authority, and contextual review. | Tests ordinary work burden, contestability, power asymmetries, access, correction, and maintenance outside the authors' own setting. No partner, consent, mandate, or deployment commitment is currently assumed. |

P0 and synthetic P1 preparation can begin as exploratory work. P2 and P3 depend on suitable material and access; neither needs to wait for a broad platform implementation. P4 follows satisfactory protected trials and an explicit partner arrangement. Pilot ordering can change with capacity, while preserving governance prerequisites and the integrated coverage below.

The first release package consists of candidate clauses, a declared minimal profile, versioned fixtures, a worked recording-to-presentation example, correction/contestation and access exercises, a pilot report, and a known-limitations register. A field recording claims, an output log, or a graph screenshot alone is not evidence for the integrated package.

## 6. Scenario and failure-case coverage

**Role:** Make proposed requirements reviewable through concrete cases without pre-deciding whether epistemic elements become ontology classes or profiles.

| Case | Exercise | Evidence sought and failure to detect |
|---|---|---|
| Fact and claimed fact | In P1, two participants give incompatible accounts of whether a meeting occurred; an institution accepts one for a specified purpose. | Record and display the assertions, sources, methods, uncertainty, and scoped institutional judgment separately. Failure: an accepted or signed assertion is displayed as unqualified truth. |
| Disputed identity | Two archive fragments in P3 appear to concern the same person or event; another account disputes the linkage. | Coexisting attributed identity hypotheses, link rationale, and reversible correction of an erroneous merge. Failure: canonicalization erases the dispute or silently propagates the mistaken identity. |
| Uncertain or conflicting evidence | P1/P3 combines direct testimony, inference, an unavailable source, and incompatible confidence assessments. | Explicit evidentiary relationships and appropriately scoped uncertainty without a fabricated common probability scale. Failure: missing evidence becomes evidence of non-occurrence or low numerical confidence becomes an objective fact. |
| Generative change and unrealized alternatives | In P0/P2 an encounter introduces a previously unused distinction, opens a research option, and closes another under a constraint. | Account of the changed options, conditions, contributors, and limits of the retrospective interpretation. Failure: options are presented as equally feasible, exhaustive, or directly observed when they were reconstructed. |
| Category revision | P1 revises an eligibility category after a challenge; P3 reinterprets an earlier classification. | Identify the category version and affected judgments, distinguish earlier recognition from later interpretation, and expose migration effects. Failure: old decisions appear to have used the revised category. |
| Restricted, missing, or destroyed data | P3 contains a restricted source, a never-collected record, and a known destruction event. | Distinguish the states only to the extent their disclosure is safe and supported; preserve uncertainty when the state is unknown. Failure: restriction is presented as nonexistence, or a marker exposes sensitive membership or existence. |
| Correction, deletion, and withdrawal | P1 includes a mistaken attribution and a request for correction or deletion under the chosen scenario procedure. | Route the request, record authority and disposition, correct derived views, and test allowed retention/deletion consequences. No default immutable copy of sensitive original content; minimal audit information or tombstones only where justified and safe. Failure: history preservation silently defeats an applicable deletion decision. |
| Presentation and public access | Render P1/P3 for an authorized reviewer and a public reader. | Disclose selection, redaction, reconstruction, uncertainty, and revision where appropriate; test whether a reader can distinguish an account from its evidentiary basis. Failure: search, export, graph labels, or cached views disclose restricted information or conceal consequential mediation. |
| Procedure interruption and retry | Interrupt a correction or access change, retry a message, and deliver an older update after a newer decision in a protocol experiment. | Defined authorization, ordering/conflict handling, acknowledgments, failure reporting, and recovery for the declared protocol. Failure: retries duplicate consequential actions or an old message restores withdrawn access. |

These exercises document both successful behavior and cases the chosen profile cannot represent. Unresolved differences remain visible rather than being normalized into one authoritative history.

## 7. Pilot evaluation and conformance boundaries

**Role:** Identify what evidence means before collecting it.

Each pilot report records its question, publication/artifact versions, participants and reviewer relationships, evidence provenance, data permissions, actions taken, failures, recording burden, and resulting draft changes. A traceability table links proposed requirement or protocol step to scenario, evidence, reviewer finding, disposition, and affected version. Unassessed requirements remain explicitly unassessed.

Evaluation covers:

- **Representation:** Can a reviewer locate the actor, claim, evidence, method, perspective, uncertainty, temporal scope, alternative, and revision relevant to the scenario without conflating them?
- **Generativity:** Can the account explain the recorded changes in conditions, relations, distinctions, or possible continuations, while identifying the evidence and limits of that explanation?
- **Practical use:** Can participants submit, contest, correct, restrict, and revisit records through the declared procedure? What expertise, time, cognitive burden, and inaccessible steps were observed?
- **Presentation:** Can readers distinguish source material from reconstruction, earlier from later judgment, and absence from restricted knowledge? Reader misunderstandings count as findings even when serialization validates.
- **Protection:** Do the exercised access, export, correction, deletion, retention, and failure paths have the stated effects, including derived views and known copies under the pilot's control?
- **Maintenance:** Can a later editor explain and apply a version change without losing the declared meaning or recreating content that was intentionally removed?

Interchange claims need additional evidence when GR-130 or a related Specification is ready. The proposed test uses independently developed implementations of the declared sender/receiver roles, with independence documented. Two datasets processed by the same implementation are insufficient. Exchange exercises cover preserved meaning, permitted loss, identifiers, epistemic scope, restriction metadata, revisions, invalid input, and version mismatch. Round trips are useful only where the format claims reversible exchange; no requirement for byte identity is presumed. Independent software implementations are not a prerequisite for a vocabulary or organizational procedure that makes no software-interoperability claim.

Conformance targets remain distinct: a record, dataset/package, recorder, presentation tool, protocol participant, organizational procedure, and preservation service have different observable obligations. A claim identifies its target, edition/profile, applicable requirements, exclusions, evidence, and assessment method. Automated schema checks establish only the constraints they actually check; human procedural or semantic review is reported separately.

A bounded conformance result does not establish the truth of an assertion, completeness of history, moral legitimacy, trustworthiness of an institution, beneficial generativity, or legal validity. Nor does an internal pilot establish certification or independent assurance. Evidence for those claims would require separately justified methods and authority.

## 8. Participant protection and contextual review

**Role:** Plan bounded review before real data use; this section does not give legal advice or interpret a jurisdiction's law.

Pilot preparation identifies the institutional context, intended purpose, affected participants and third parties, required data, access authority, permissions or consent where applicable, custody, retention, and available routes to challenge a record. Consent is not assumed to resolve every institutional or third-party issue. Synthetic and minimized materials provide an initial route when authority or protection is unresolved.

Before P4 or other sensitive real-data use, the proposed contextual review covers applicable correction/deletion/access duties, confidentiality, contribution and reuse permissions, records-management duties, participant vulnerability, and any cross-institution or cross-border handling actually planned. A qualified reviewer evaluates the identified jurisdiction and activity. Findings identify their scope, assumptions, unresolved conflicts, and limits; they are not generalized to other institutions.

The pilot plan also identifies who can pause collection or publication, restrict affected material, notify responsible participants, investigate a problem, and authorize resumption. Public pilot reports use appropriate minimization. Technical auditability does not require a public or permanent sensitive event history; even hashes, identifiers, and deletion markers receive review for disclosure risk.

## 9. Decisions to resolve before execution

**Role:** Provide inputs to the programme's [open-questions register](open_questions.md) and subsequent governance decisions.

- Who participates in the founding decision, and what authority and limits are declared?
- Which responsibilities can be staffed now, and how are independent appeals and material conflicts handled with that capacity?
- What review periods, participation expectations, consensus criteria, and exceptional procedures are proportionate and credible?
- Which publication, software, schema, and contribution licensing terms support the programme's intended openness?
- What is the bounded first integrated profile, and which candidate assertions or generativity-related descriptions remain experimental?
- Which pilot materials are available with appropriate permissions, and which can safely be public?
- What evidence is sufficient for each proposed conformance claim, and what recording burden is acceptable to the affected participants?
- Which correction, deletion, retention, and access tensions require contextual legal review or a separate policy proposal rather than a universal technical rule?

The next concrete governance deliverable is a reviewable launch package with tracked alternatives and unresolved choices. The next concrete experimental deliverable is the P0/P1 integrated scenario package. Neither is represented here as completed, adopted, or externally endorsed.
