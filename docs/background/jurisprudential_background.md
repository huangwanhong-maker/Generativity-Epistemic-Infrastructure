# Jurisprudential Background

**Status:** Informative / preliminary

## 1. Purpose

This document identifies legal and jurisprudential questions that arise when generativity records move from research or archival tools into institutional practice.

## 2. Core distinction

A technical standard can define data structures, procedures, and conformance conditions.

It does not by itself determine:

- legal validity;
- legal responsibility;
- evidentiary admissibility;
- rights and duties;
- remedies;
- jurisdiction.

Those questions require jurisprudential and legal analysis.

## 3. Areas for development

Potential areas include:

- evidentiary status of generativity records;
- provenance and attribution;
- correction and reply rights;
- preservation duties;
- privacy and confidentiality;
- institutional accountability;
- due process in automated or AI-mediated decision histories;
- legal treatment of contested or incomplete records;
- archival duties toward future publics;
- legal recognition of heterogeneous participation.

## 4. Jurisprudential questions

The programme should ask, among other things:

- When may a record be relied upon?
- What does procedural fairness require when a record is disputed?
- Who may correct, annotate, restrict, or challenge a record?
- When does preservation conflict with privacy or erasure interests?
- How should legal institutions distinguish observed facts from interpretations or attributions?
- How should legal responsibility be handled when human and computational participation are deeply entangled?

## 5. Relation to standards

These questions may lead to:

- technical requirements;
- implementation guidance;
- legal profiles;
- policy proposals;
- model institutional procedures.

They should not be resolved by ontology design alone.
