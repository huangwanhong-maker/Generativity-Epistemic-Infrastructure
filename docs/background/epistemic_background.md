# Epistemic Background

**Status:** Informative / evolving

## 1. Problem

Generativity records are not direct copies of reality.

They are produced through observation, selection, interpretation, categorization, memory, measurement, documentation, and later reconstruction.

The standards programme therefore requires an explicit epistemic layer.

## 2. Candidate distinctions

The programme should preserve distinctions among:

- Trace
- Observation
- Claim
- Evidence
- Interpretation
- Judgment
- Attribution
- Retrospective interpretation

These categories are provisional and may become classes, relations, profiles, or metadata structures.

## 3. Epistemic provenance

A record should be capable of expressing:

- who or what produced the observation;
- by which method;
- at what time;
- from which source;
- under which interpretive frame;
- with what uncertainty;
- whether it is direct, inferred, reconstructed, or disputed;
- whether later evidence modified its status.

## 4. Contestation

Different observers may maintain incompatible interpretations of the same underlying traces.

The standard should support plurality without requiring immediate collapse into a single canonical narrative.

## 5. Historical revision

Later interpretations should not erase earlier epistemic states.

A record system should be capable of representing:

- what was believed at time t1;
- what evidence was available at t1;
- what changed at t2;
- how the interpretation was revised.

## 6. Epistemic justice

The programme should remain attentive to asymmetries in whose observations are recorded, whose categories become authoritative, whose records survive, and whose interpretations become institutionally visible.

Detailed normative treatment belongs in later standards and technical reports.
