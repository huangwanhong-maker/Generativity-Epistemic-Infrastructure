# Readiness and burden assessment

**Class:** Informative assessment design and evidence-gap record  
**Status:** PROVISIONAL; Working Draft 0.2 validation cycle  
**Boundary:** Repository evidence and synthetic exercises; no external institution assessed

## 1. What the available evidence establishes

The programme has authored governance and technical drafts, preserved their earlier editions, compiled review artifacts and performed bounded editorial and arithmetic checks. These are preparation activities. They do not establish accepted appointments, stakeholder representation, legal authority, rights clearance, independent review, operational accessibility or successful repair of a real consequence.

The following are evidence gaps in this dossier, not assertions that the relevant people or capacities cannot exist.

| Readiness question | Evidence in this cycle | Present conclusion | Next evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Can a founding authority adopt and resource the programme? | Proposed Charter and unsigned founding template | Not established by the dossier | Identified authority, applicable mandate, accepted responsibilities and resource commitments |
| Can separate and competent review actually be supplied? | D1, TC and ethics requirements; fictional reviewer reassignment | Not assessed | Accepted reviewers, conflicts record, availability and an observed handling exercise |
| Are affected interests able to participate? | Participation requirements and synthetic access barriers | Not assessed | Recruitment and access evidence, actual contributions, missing perspectives and response records |
| Are contribution and publication rights usable? | Proposed rights-clearance procedures | Not established by a procedural draft | Artifact-specific terms, authority to grant them and resolved source restrictions |
| Can a practice capture a meaningful history at reasonable cost? | Authored P1 record inventory and selected component assessment | Expressibility examined; operational burden not measured | Repeated capture/review tasks with time, assistance, omission and interruption records |
| Can the challenge and correction routes work? | Declared synthetic routes and failure branches | Real procedure not assessed | Observed receipt, assignment, response, decision, notification and downstream correction |
| Can intended users understand the result? | Editorial PDF inspection and authored public view | User understanding not assessed | Task-based sessions with intended users and suitable access arrangements |
| Does the infrastructure improve outcomes? | Proposed causal accounts and unresolved practical repair in P1 | No observed effectiveness finding | A bounded outcome question, comparison, time horizon, alternate explanations and follow-up evidence |

## 2. Burden dimensions to measure

Counting clauses, records or fields does not measure minutes of work, cognitive effort or institutional cost. The 753 baseline identifiers are a review inventory; they are not 753 mandatory independent entries in every record. Conditional obligations, reusable package-level declarations and overlapping evidence need explicit treatment.

A future observed rehearsal can record the following without inventing a universal acceptable threshold:

| Dimension | Observation record | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Initial setup | Roles involved, declaration work, configuration and review time | Separate reusable setup from each subsequent claim or case |
| Capture | Active task time, material available, fields requiring judgment, assistance and interruptions | Missing historical evidence is not necessarily a usability failure |
| Assessment | Time to locate grounds, understand source dependence, identify an uncertainty and apply a criterion | Fast assessment can still be wrong or superficial |
| Contestation | Steps and effort to submit, clarify, respond and obtain a reasoned disposition | Successful submission is not successful remedy |
| Protection | Work to prepare permitted views, inspect access, manage retention and document limitations | Reduced effort does not justify exposing protected content |
| Revision | Dependent records located, transformations checked, notifications attempted and verified | An uncontrolled external copy can remain uncorrected |
| Participation | Language/access assistance, preparation burden, unavailable perspectives and withdrawal | Attendance alone does not establish effective influence |
| Maintenance | Recurring custody, review, migration, incident and training effort | One initial build does not establish sustainable capacity |

Each observation identifies the task, actual participant role, experience level, scenario/version, start/end convention, excluded idle time, interruptions, assistance, result and limitation. Thresholds or comparison criteria are selected before interpreting measurements and remain purpose-specific. This cycle has no such participant measurements and reports none.

## 3. Profile decisions informed by the cycle

Three assessment subjects need separate consideration in GR-150 development: a retained documentary package, the practice that creates and maintains it, and a procedure handling challenges and practical responses. Version 0.2 permits partial documentary assessment but supplies no reduced profile that turns it into complete practice or procedure conformity.

Candidate simplifications include reusing an unchanged package declaration, referencing controlled material rather than duplicating it, providing explicitly permitted unknown historical context, and omitting a conditionally required feature when its trigger is absent. They do not permit dropping known material loss, concealing an unassessed component, or calling an unperformed action inapplicable.

A profile proposal records parent obligations, intended users, trigger rules, retained safeguards, additional obligations, exclusions, evidence methods and its exact claim. Alternatives are assessed against the same scenario. There is no adopted profile in this cycle and no requirement to use a particular database or schema.

## 4. Readiness consequence

The current evidence supports a traced drafting revision and more precise rehearsal materials. Formal governance adoption and claims about institutional performance remain pending the evidence above. Preparatory drafting can continue, with its status and assumptions explicit, while those capacities are developed.
