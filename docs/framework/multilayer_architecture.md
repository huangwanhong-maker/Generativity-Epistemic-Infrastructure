# Multilayer Architecture of the Generativity Standards Program

**Status:** PROVISIONAL  
**Purpose:** Expand the programme beyond a narrow ontology-and-data-model approach.

## 1. Rationale

A generativity record cannot be adequately designed by specifying recordable beings or structures alone.

The programme must also address:

- how a phenomenon becomes knowable;
- how observations, claims, interpretations, and judgments are distinguished;
- how evidence is generated, transmitted, contested, and revised;
- how normative significance is assigned;
- how law and jurisprudence recognize, classify, protect, or constrain generative relations;
- how institutions use generativity records in governance;
- how political and economic structures shape what can be generated, preserved, recognized, or returned.

Accordingly, ontology is only one layer of the programme.

## 2. Proposed layers

### L0 — Generative metamodel

Concerned with highly abstract formal dimensions such as:

- change;
- structure;
- constraint;
- difference.

### L1 — Record ontology

Candidate operational classes include:

- Entity;
- State;
- Event;
- Process;
- Relation;
- Property.

These remain open to extension.

### L2 — Epistemic layer

Concerned with how a record comes to be known, asserted, interpreted, and revised.

Candidate concepts include:

- Observation;
- Trace;
- Claim;
- Evidence;
- Interpretation;
- Judgment;
- Attribution;
- Perspective;
- Confidence;
- Uncertainty;
- Contestation;
- Revision;
- Retrospective reconstruction.

The epistemic layer should prevent a record from collapsing occurrence, observation, and interpretation into one field.

### L3 — Normative and ethical layer

Concerned with:

- value;
- harm;
- benefit;
- justice;
- fairness;
- recognition;
- responsibility;
- generativity return;
- preservation obligations;
- competing claims over records and their uses.

This layer should distinguish descriptive records from normative evaluations.

### L4 — Jurisprudential and legal layer

Concerned with how generativity-related records interact with legal concepts and procedures.

Potential topics include:

- legal status of records;
- admissibility and evidentiary weight;
- authorship and contribution;
- attribution and provenance;
- rights of access, correction, objection, and reply;
- privacy and data protection;
- confidentiality and privilege;
- retention and destruction;
- procedural fairness;
- institutional accountability;
- legal recognition of heterogeneous human/AI participation;
- preservation of contested histories.

The programme should avoid assuming that a technical standard itself creates legal rights or duties.

### L5 — Institutional and governance layer

Concerned with how organizations use records in:

- research governance;
- public administration;
- international cooperation;
- education;
- cultural heritage;
- scientific collaboration;
- AI governance;
- institutional memory;
- accountability and review.

### L6 — Political-economy layer

Concerned with:

- ownership and control of generative infrastructure;
- extraction of value from generative relations;
- asymmetries in capacity to record and preserve;
- concentration of archival power;
- access to knowledge infrastructure;
- generativity return;
- resource allocation;
- institutional incentives.

### L7 — Technical implementation layer

Concerned with:

- information models;
- identifiers;
- graph structures;
- serialization;
- APIs;
- validation;
- interoperability;
- cryptographic integrity;
- versioning.

## 3. Cross-layer principle

The layers should interact without being collapsed.

For example:

- an Event record is ontological/operational;
- a statement that the Event occurred is epistemic;
- a judgment that the Event was unjust is normative;
- a finding that it violated a legal duty is jurisprudential;
- a requirement that an institution preserve the record is governance/legal;
- a JSON representation of the record is technical.

These should remain distinguishable even when represented together.

## 4. Standards implication

The programme should develop coordinated standards rather than one ontology-only specification.

A future conformance claim may need to state which layers it implements.

## 5. Political and institutional proposals

The programme may also publish policy proposals for international organizations and other public-interest institutions.

Those proposals should be grounded in the standards work but remain a separate publication class.

A standards document defines interoperable requirements.

A policy proposal may recommend institutional adoption, pilot programmes, governance arrangements, capacity-building, or legal/policy experimentation.

The two should reference each other without being conflated.
